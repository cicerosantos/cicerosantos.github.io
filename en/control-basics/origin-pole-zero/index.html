Pole and zero at the origin: pure integrator and pure differentiator

The pure integrator ωi/s\omega_i/sωi​/s, a pole at the origin, and the pure differentiator s/ωis/\omega_is/ωi​, a zero at the origin, are a single line on the Bode plot, ∓20 dB per decade over the whole axis, crossing 0 dB at fif_ifi​, with the phase constant at ∓90°; multiplied by them, a function's whole Bode plot tilts.

A pure integrator is a pole at the origin, ωi/s\omega_i/sωi​/s; a pure differentiator is a zero at the origin, s/ωis/\omega_is/ωi​. "Pure" says the factor stands alone: no other pole or zero, and a single constant, ωi\omega_iωi​, which absorbs any gain. The PI (PI, phase lead and the type III), KI (1+s/ωz)/sK_I\,(1 + s/\omega_z)/sKI​(1+s/ωz​)/s, is a pure integrator times a zero. The pole sits exactly at s=0s = 0s=0: a pole near the origin, 1/(1+s/ωp)1/(1 + s/\omega_p)1/(1+s/ωp​), is a leaky integrator, flat below fpf_pfp​.

One line over the whole axis

At s=jωs = j\omegas=jω, the pure integrator is ωi/(jω)=−j fi/f\omega_i/(j\omega) = -j\,f_i/fωi​/(jω)=−jfi​/f:

  • the magnitude is fi/ff_i/ffi​/f, or 20log⁡10(fi/f)20\log_{10}(f_i/f)20log10​(fi​/f) dB: a line of −20 dB per decade at every frequency, through 0 dB at fif_ifi​;
  • the phase is −90° at every frequency, because −j-j−j is purely imaginary.

The pure differentiator is the mirror: j f/fij\,f/f_ijf/fi​, a line of +20 dB per decade through 0 dB at fif_ifi​, with a constant phase of +90°.

Here the asymptote is the curve itself: there is no corner, no 3 dB error and no phase line. fif_ifi​ is not a corner: it is just where the line crosses 0 dB. The factor never tends to 1, so the phase rule of First-order pole and zero has no end to anchor the phase to: it stays at ∓90°, unchanged.

Reading fif_ifi​ off the plot

Extend the ∓20 dB per decade line to 0 dB: the crossing is fif_ifi​. If it falls off the plot, use any point: if the pure integrator's line is at LLL dB at fff, then fi=f⋅10L/20f_i = f \cdot 10^{L/20}fi​=f⋅10L/20; for the differentiator, fi=f⋅10−L/20f_i = f \cdot 10^{-L/20}fi​=f⋅10−L/20.

The pure integrator tilts everything

Multiplying any function by ωi/s\omega_i/sωi​/s adds the pure integrator's line, in dB: every stretch of the asymptote loses 20 dB per decade, and every phase value loses 90°. A flat level becomes a −20 dB per decade descent; a −20 dB per decade descent becomes −40. That is why, in a function with a pole at the origin, the low-frequency asymptote is not flat: it already starts sloped, and the flat level that would give the DC gain does not exist. With NNN pure integrators, the initial slope is −20N-20N−20N dB per decade, and the phase starts at −90N-90N−90N°.

The pure integrator is not BIBO stable (Transfer function and factored form): the pole sits on the jωj\omegajω axis, not to its left, and a step at the input gives a ramp that never stops. The pure differentiator is improper, like the zero of First-order pole and zero.
