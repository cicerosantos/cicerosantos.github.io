From margin to damping: the ζ ≈ MF/100 rule and where it fails

How the phase margin turns into damping and overshoot, over what range the ζ ≈ MF/100 rule holds, and why it misses in the buck with the 2p2z.

First, see: Feedback and margins, Second-order pole pair and quality factor Q

The phase margin says how far the loop is from oscillating (Feedback and margins). What the oscilloscope shows, though, is the step: how far it overshoots the target and for how long it rings. This note bridges the two, and shows in the buck where the bridge gives way.

From margin to step

Hence the rule ζ≈MF/100\zeta \approx \mathrm{MF}/100ζ≈MF/100, with MF in degrees. For the integrator-plus-pole loop, it is off by less than 10 % between 37° and 67° of margin, and by less than 15 % at any margin up to 71°; the error vanishes at 57.4°. Below that, the rule promises a bit more damping than there is; above it, a bit less. This range belongs to the model loop, not to any loop.

A real loop has more poles. The pair near the crossover is dominant when the others are at least five times farther from the jωj\omegajω axis (ten, to trust the numbers) and no zero sits near it: then the step rings near fcf_cfc​ with the overshoot of the table. When a pole lies closer to the axis than the pair, that pole governs the slow response.

In the converters

In the buck, with the 2p2z, the loop crosses at 8 kHz with an MF of 41° (Loop gain, crossover frequency and margins).

Damping. Predict: with an MF of 41°, the rule gives ζ≈0.41\zeta \approx 0.41ζ≈0.41, Q≈1.2Q \approx 1.2Q≈1.2 and about 25 % overshoot. Check: the closed-loop model gives a pair at 7.2 kHz with ζ=0.31\zeta = 0.31ζ=0.31 (Q≈1.6Q \approx 1.6Q≈1.6, overshoot of 36 %): the rule promises a third more damping than there is. In QSPICE, the load step from 1 to 2 A drops the output to 8.59 V, and it comes back to 9.14 V (The 2p2z compensator): the return is 35 % of the dip, which is what ζ≈0.31\zeta \approx 0.31ζ≈0.31 predicts.

Why the rule misses here. It assumes two things, and the buck meets neither. First, that the loop near the crossover is an integrator followed by a pole; the buck's is the plant near −180°, plus the compensator's lead and the firmware's delay. Not even the exact formula of the table gets it right: at 41.3°, it gives ζ=0.38\zeta = 0.38ζ=0.38. Second, that the pair is dominant. The buck's closed loop has, besides the pair, a real pole near 470 Hz, closer to the jωj\omegajω axis than the pair: by the criterion above, the pair is not dominant. The rule still gives the order of magnitude of the ringing near fcf_cfc​, but not the number, and it says nothing about the slow pole.

The slow pole shows in the load step as a tail: by the model, a tail of 5 to 6 mV remains after the ringing, with a time constant of about 0.34 ms, and it stays above 1 mV for about 1 ms after the step. In QSPICE, that tail is lost in the tens of millivolts of fluctuation of the simulated output. In the reference step, the same pole dominates: the output has no overshoot, and it takes more than 1 ms to reach 5 V. In QSPICE, it is still 0.46 V above 5 V 600 µs after the start of the ramp, and 0.14 V at 1 ms (5 µs averages).

Exercise

Predict, then check on the figure. The aggressive design has an MF of 16° at 18 kHz. Before looking at the step: by the rule, what is the estimated ζ\zetaζ, the overshoot of the pair and the period of the ringing?

Answer

By the rule, ζ≈0.16\zeta \approx 0.16ζ≈0.16; the overshoot would be e−πζ/1−ζ2≈60e^{-\pi\zeta/\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}} \approx 60e−πζ/1−ζ2​≈60 %; the ringing would be near 18 kHz, with a period of about 55 µs. The closed-loop model gives a pair at 19.9 kHz with ζ=0.14\zeta = 0.14ζ=0.14, an overshoot of 64 % and a period of 50 µs, the same as read in QSPICE. With little margin, the rule misses less than in the 2p2z (0.16 against 0.14), but here there is also a slow real pole, near 900 Hz, and the pair is not dominant. In the figure, the output makes several quick oscillations after the step, and the reading below it gives the settling time.
