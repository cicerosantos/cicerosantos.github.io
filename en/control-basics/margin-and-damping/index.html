From margin to damping: the ζ ≈ PM/100 rule and where it fails

How the phase margin turns into damping and overshoot, over what range the ζ ≈ PM/100 rule holds, and why it misses in the buck with the 2p2z.

First, see: Feedback and margins, Second-order pair and quality factor Q

The phase margin says how far the loop is from oscillating (Feedback and margins). What the oscilloscope shows, though, is the step: how far it overshoots the target and for how long it rings. This note bridges the two, and shows in the buck where the bridge gives way.

From margin to step

Hence the rule ζ≈PM/100\zeta \approx \mathrm{PM}/100ζ≈PM/100, with PM in degrees. In the integrator-plus-pole loop, it is off by less than 10 % between 37° and 67.5° of margin and by less than 15 % at any margin up to 71°; the error vanishes at 57.4°. Below that, the rule promises a little more damping than there is; above, a little less. That range belongs to the model loop, not to any loop. A real loop has more poles. The pair near the crossover is dominant when the others are at least five times further from the jωj\omegajω axis (ten, to trust the numbers) and no zero sits near it: then the step rings near fcf_cfc​ with the table's overshoot. When a pole sits closer to the axis than the pair, it rules the slow response.

In the converters

In the buck, with the 2p2z, the loop crosses at 8 kHz with a PM of 41° (Loop gain, crossover frequency and margins).

Damping. Predict: with a PM of 41°, the rule gives ζ≈0.41\zeta \approx 0.41ζ≈0.41, Q≈1.2Q \approx 1.2Q≈1.2 and about 25 % overshoot. Check: the closed-loop model gives a pair at 7.2 kHz with ζ=0.31\zeta = 0.31ζ=0.31 (Q≈1.6Q \approx 1.6Q≈1.6, 35 % overshoot): the rule promises a third more damping than there is. In QSPICE, the 1 to 2 A load step drops the output to 8.59 V, and it swings back to 9.14 V (The 2p2z compensator): the swing back is 35 % of the dip, what ζ≈0.31\zeta \approx 0.31ζ≈0.31 predicts.

Why the rule misses here. It assumes two things, and the buck meets neither. First, that the loop near the crossover is an integrator followed by a pole; the buck's is the plant near −180°, plus the compensator's lead and the firmware's delay. Not even the table's exact formula gets it right: with 41.3°, it gives ζ=0.38\zeta = 0.38ζ=0.38. Second, that the pair is dominant. Besides the pair, the buck's closed loop has a real pole near 470 Hz, closer to the jωj\omegajω axis than the pair: by the criterion above, the pair is not dominant. The rule still gives the order of magnitude of the ringing near fcf_cfc​, but not the number, and it says nothing about the slow pole.

The rule does not see everything

The buck's 2p2z leaves ∣TL∣|T_L|∣TL​∣ slightly below 1 between 1 and 2.4 kHz, where its zeros are. The margin does not see that. From the model, without the ADC's quantization, a slow tail of a few millivolts (5 to 6 mV at its peak) is left after the ringing; it decays with a time constant of about 0.34 ms and stays above 1 mV until about 1 ms after the step. It is the real closed-loop pole near 470 Hz. In QSPICE, it is lost in the simulated output's fluctuation of tens of millivolts. In the reference step, the same pole is no detail: the step has no overshoot at all, and the output takes more than 1 ms to reach 5 V. In QSPICE, it is still 0.45 V above 5 V 600 µs after the ramp starts, and 0.14 V at 1 ms.

Exercise

Predict, then check in the figure. The aggressive design has a PM of 16° at 18 kHz. Before looking at the step: by the rule, what are the estimated ζ\zetaζ, the pair's overshoot and the ringing's period?

Answer

By the rule, ζ≈0.16\zeta \approx 0.16ζ≈0.16; the overshoot would be e−πζ/1−ζ2≈60e^{-\pi\zeta/\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}} \approx 60e−πζ/1−ζ2​≈60 %; the ringing would sit near 18 kHz, with a period of about 55 µs. The closed-loop model gives a pair at 19.8 kHz with ζ=0.14\zeta = 0.14ζ=0.14, 64 % overshoot and a 50 µs period, the same one QSPICE shows. With little margin, the rule misses less than with the 2p2z (0.16 against 0.14), but here too there are slow real poles, near 900 Hz, and the pair is not dominant. In the figure, the output rings through several fast cycles after the step, and the readout below it gives the settling time.
