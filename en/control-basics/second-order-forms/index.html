Second-order forms: zeros, undamped, inverted and right-half-plane

The pole pair has nine variants: the zero pair, the undamped limit, the forms in ω0/s\omega_0/sω0​/s and the right-half-plane ones, and each is the ordinary pair with a mirror, a sign or a double integrator.

The inverted zero pair is a double integrator (−40 dB per decade, 0 dB at f0f_0f0​, −180°) times an ordinary zero pair: the magnitude falls 40 dB per decade up to f0f_0f0​ and stays at 0 dB after it, and the phase goes from −180° to 0°. The inverted pole pair is the reciprocal, a double differentiator times the pole pair: +40 dB per decade up to f0f_0f0​, then 0 dB, and the phase from +180° to 0°. The phase is 0° at high frequency, where the factor tends to 1. On the s-plane, the inverted form has, besides the pair, a double pole or zero at the origin.

In the right half-plane: the sign of the middle term

1−sQω0+(sω0)21 - \frac{s}{Q\omega_0} + \left(\frac{s}{\omega_0}\right)^21−Qω0​s​+(ω0​s​)2

The roots are those of the ordinary pair mirrored across the jωj\omegajω axis, with real part +ω0/(2Q)+\omega_0/(2Q)+ω0​/(2Q). At s=jωs = j\omegas=jω, the factor is 1−x2−jx/Q1 - x^2 - jx/Q1−x2−jx/Q: the real part is the same, and only the imaginary part changes sign. The magnitude is identical to the ordinary pair's, and the phase turns the other way. The RHP zero pair goes from 0° to −180°: it has a zero pair's magnitude and a pole pair's phase. It is a non-minimum-phase form, with more lag than the magnitude suggests. The RHP pole pair goes from 0° to +180° and is unstable.

The inverted RHP forms combine both rules. In the zero pair, the double integrator gives −180°, and the RHP pair, 0° to −180°: the sum goes from −180° to −360°. The factor tends to 1 at high frequency, so the curve shifts by 360° and goes from +180° to 0°. The inverted RHP pole pair goes from −180° to 0°.
