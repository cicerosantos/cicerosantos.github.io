Classical control

The first four units of classical control, in order: reading the plant in frequency, closing the loop, with the margins and the limit of a right-half-plane zero, compensating, with steady-state error, phase lead and phase lag, and reading the root locus.

This trail gathers the first four units of classical control, in order. In the first, you read a converter's plant as a transfer function, in factored form, and draw its Bode plot by asymptotes. In the second, you close the loop, measure the margins, and see the limit that a right-half-plane zero puts on the crossover. In the third, you choose the compensator: the integrator for the steady-state error, phase lead for the margin, and phase lag when reducing the error is enough. In the fourth, you see the same limits in the sss plane, through the path of the closed-loop poles, and what a cancellation hides. Each step uses the lab converters' numbers. If you do not know where to start, answer the questions in Start here first.

Unit 1: reading the plant

  1. Transfer function and factored form: the buck's plant as a ratio of polynomials, with the DC gain, the poles and the zeros in factored form.
  2. First-order pole and zero: the simplest block, a real pole or zero, with its asymptotes and the 3 dB error at the corner.
  3. Second-order pole pair and quality factor Q: two poles together can oscillate, and the factor QQQ says how much; it is the buck's LC.
  4. Second-order filters: the catalog: the five second-order filters, from low-pass to all-pass, recognized by their asymptotes.
  5. Bode by composition: from factored form to asymptote: add the asymptotes of each factor to draw the plant's Bode plot, and read the equation back from the curve.
  6. From circuit to factored form (reinforcement): optional reinforcement, to see where the plant's formulas come from in the circuit.

Unit 2: closing the loop

  1. Feedback and margins: with the loop closed, the sensitivity 1/(1+T)1/(1 + T)1/(1+T), the last crossover, the margins and what they bound in the peak of ∣S∣|S|∣S∣.
  2. From margin to damping: the ζ ≈ MF/100 rule and where it fails: from phase margin to the damping of the step, with the rule ζ≈MF/100\zeta \approx \mathrm{MF}/100ζ≈MF/100, the range where it holds and where it fails in the buck.
  3. Right-half-plane zero: the right-half-plane zero as a phase lag, the initial inverse response, and the limit on the crossover in the boost.

Unit 3: compensating

  1. Steady-state error and system type: the system type and the error constants, the ceiling of a proportional controller on a type 0 plant, and the integrator that zeroes the step error.
  2. PI, phase lead and the type III: the PI, phase lead and the type III, which the buck's 2p2z implements.
  3. The phase-lag compensator: phase lag, which multiplies the low-frequency gain without moving the crossover, and why it does not zero the error.

Unit 4: the root locus

  1. Root locus (qualitative reading): how the closed-loop poles move with the gain; the delay sets the buck's ceiling, and the right-half-plane zero sets the boost's.
  2. Pole-zero cancellation and its risks: what a zero placed on a pole hides: the pair stays in the closed loop, the doublet leaves a slow tail, and a right-half-plane zero is never canceled.
