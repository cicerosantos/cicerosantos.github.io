Closing and compensating the loop

Units 2 to 4 of classical control: the loop, the margins, the transient and the right-half-plane zero; steady-state error, phase lead and phase lag; the root locus.

Start the trail →

This trail gathers units 2 to 4 of classical control, in order. It starts from unit 1, the From Laplace to Bode trail, which teaches reading the plant from its Bode plot. In unit 2, the loop is closed, the margins are measured and translated into the transient, and a right-half-plane zero limits the crossover. In unit 3, the compensator is chosen: the integrator for the steady-state error, phase lead for the margin, and phase lag when reducing the error is enough. In unit 4, the same limits appear in the sss-plane, through the path of the closed-loop poles, along with what a cancellation hides. Each step uses the numbers of the lab's converters. To know where to start, answer the questions in Start here first.

Unit 3: compensating

  1. Steady-state error and system type: the system type and the error constants, the ceiling of a proportional controller on a type 0 plant, and the integrator that zeroes the step error.
  2. PI, phase lead and the type III: the PI, also in inverted form, phase lead and the type III, which the buck's 2p2z implements.
  3. The phase-lag compensator: phase lag, which multiplies the low-frequency gain without moving the crossover, and why it does not zero the error.

Unit 4: the root locus

  1. Root locus (qualitative reading): how the closed-loop poles move with the gain; the delay sets the buck's ceiling, and the right-half-plane zero sets the boost's.
  2. Pole-zero cancellation and its risks: what a zero placed on a pole hides: the pair stays in the closed loop, the doublet leaves a slow tail, and a right-half-plane zero is never canceled.
