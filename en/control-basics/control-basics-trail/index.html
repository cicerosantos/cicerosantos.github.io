Control basics

Seven notes, in order, from what a transfer function is to the buck's compensator: poles, zeros, Q, margins, the right-half-plane zero and compensators.

This trail is the course's control refresher, to take in one go instead of note by note. Each step uses the numbers of the lab's converters. If you do not know where to begin, first answer the questions in Start here.

  1. Transfer function, poles and zeros: the buck's plant as a ratio of polynomials, with the DC gain, the poles and the zeros in standard form.
  2. First-order pole and zero: the simplest block, a real pole or zero, in the Bode plot and in the step.
  3. Second-order pair and quality factor Q: two poles together can oscillate, and the factor QQQ says how much; this is the buck's LC.
  4. Feedback and margins: with the plant understood, we close the loop and measure the distance to oscillation.
  5. From margin to damping: the ζ ≈ PM/100 rule and where it fails: the margin turns into damping and overshoot, and the buck shows where the rule of thumb fails.
  6. Right-half-plane zero: the zero on the wrong side of the plane, which the boost has and the buck does not, and why it limits the crossover.
  7. PI, phase lead and the type III: the blocks the compensator puts together for zero error and phase margin.

End of the trail

From here, go back to the converters: the From the analog buck to the digital loop trail takes the buck from the power stage to the firmware, and the From the boost to the digital loop shows what changes when the plant has the right-half-plane zero.
