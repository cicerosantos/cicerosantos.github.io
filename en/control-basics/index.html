    Concepts

    • Feedback and marginsWhy close the loop, what the sensitivity 1/(1 + T_L) says and what the phase and gain margins measure.
    • First-order pole and zeroA real pole brings the magnitude down at −20 dB per decade and lags the phase to −90°; a zero does the opposite. In time, the pole is an exponential with time constant 1/ωₚ.
    • From margin to damping: the ζ ≈ PM/100 rule and where it failsHow the phase margin turns into damping and overshoot, over what range the ζ ≈ PM/100 rule holds, and why it misses in the buck with the 2p2z.
    • PI, phase lead and the type IIIThe integrator brings the steady-state error to zero and costs 90° of phase; phase lead gives phase back at the crossover; the type III is an integrator with two leads, and the 2p2z is its digital relative.
    • Right-half-plane zeroA zero right of the jω axis has an ordinary zero's magnitude and a pole's phase; it destabilises nothing, but makes the step start the wrong way and limits the crossover.
    • Second-order pair and quality factor QA pole pair is described by f₀ and Q; in the Bode plot, Q is the height of the resonant peak, and in the s-plane and the step it is the balance between oscillation and decay.
    • Transfer function, poles and zerosA transfer function is a ratio of two polynomials in s; the DC gain, the poles and the zeros, written in standard form, tell almost everything about the Bode plot and the step.
