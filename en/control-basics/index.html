Classical control

The classical control the other collections use, in four units, and the start of the course. Unit 1, the From Laplace to Bode trail, is generic: it matches a Laplace expression to its asymptotic Bode plot and goes back the other way. Units 2 to 4, the Closing and compensating the loop trail, close the loop, compensate and read the root locus, with the converters' numbers. Come in through a trail, through the Start here diagnostic, or when a note in the other collections asks for it; each note ends with the way back to the converter.

    Concepts

    • Bode by composition: from factored form to asymptoteTwo tables sum up the first- and second-order forms, and the recipe works both ways, adding up the factors of G(s)G(s)G(s) into the asymptotic Bode plot and reading G(s)G(s)G(s) back from it.
    • Butterworth: the Q of each section and the order nThe maximally flat filter, built as a cascade of pole pairs with the same f₀ and a real pole for odd orders, with the table of Q from n = 2 to 8 and a band-pass by composition.
    • Feedback and marginsHow the loop closes, what the sensitivity 1/(1 + T) says, and why the phase and gain margins bound the peak of |S|.
    • First-order pole and zeroA real pole drops the magnitude at −20 dB per decade and lags the phase down to −90°; a zero does the opposite. The asymptote is 3 dB off at the pole frequency, where the real phase is −45°.
    • From margin to damping: the ζ ≈ MF/100 rule and where it failsHow the phase margin turns into damping and overshoot, over what range the ζ ≈ MF/100 rule holds, and why it misses in the buck with the 2p2z.
    • Inverted forms and the sign on the Bode plotA factor written in ω/s\omega/sω/s is 1 at high frequency and is a pure integrator or a pure differentiator times the ordinary form, sometimes with a minus sign; with that decomposition and the ±180° rule, factor by factor, the four inverted forms are drawn by asymptotes.
    • PI, phase lead and the type IIIThe integrator brings the steady-state error to zero and costs 90° of phase; phase lead gives phase back at the crossover; the type III is an integrator with two leads, and the 2p2z is its digital relative.
    • Pole and zero at the origin: pure integrator and pure differentiatorThe pure integrator ωi/s\omega_i/sωi​/s, a pole at the origin, and the pure differentiator s/ωis/\omega_is/ωi​, a zero at the origin, are a single line on the Bode plot, ∓20 dB per decade over the whole axis, crossing 0 dB at fif_ifi​, with the phase constant at ∓90°; multiplied by them, a function's whole Bode plot tilts.
    • Pole-zero cancellation and its risksA compensator zero on a plant pole removes the pole from the loop gain, but not from the closed loop; an imperfect cancellation leaves a slow doublet, and a right-half-plane zero is never canceled.
    • Right-half-plane pole and zeroThe pole 1/(1−s/ωp)1/(1 - s/\omega_p)1/(1−s/ωp​) and the zero 1−s/ωz1 - s/\omega_z1−s/ωz​ have the magnitude of the ordinary forms and the mirrored phase, from 0° to +90° and from 0° to −90°; only the phase tells the two sides of the s-plane apart.
    • Right-half-plane zero: the effect on the loopA zero right of the jω axis has an ordinary zero's magnitude and a pole's phase; it takes phase away from the loop, makes the step start the wrong way, and limits the crossover.
    • Root locus (qualitative reading)How the closed-loop poles move as the compensator's gain rises, the rules for a hand sketch, and the reading in the buck and the boost, where the right-half-plane zero puts a ceiling on the gain.
    • Second-order filters: each one a composition of the formsThe nine second-order filters, from low-pass to all-pass, each written as a product of the forms already seen, with the asymptotes, the value at f₀ and the explorer.
    • Second-order forms: zeros, undamped, inverted and right-half-planeThe pole pair has nine variants: the zero pair, the undamped limit, the forms in ω0/s\omega_0/sω0​/s and the right-half-plane ones, and each is the ordinary pair with a mirror, a sign or a double pure integrator.
    • Second-order pole pair and quality factor QA pole pair is described by f₀ and Q. In the Bode plot, Q gives the height of the resonant peak; in the step, the balance between oscillation and decay, with the overshoot.
    • Steady-state error and system typeThe number of integrators in the loop gain, the system type, decides for which input the steady-state error goes to zero; the constants KpK_pKp​, KvK_vKv​ and KaK_aKa​ give the error left for a step, a ramp and a parabola.
    • The phase-lag compensatorA pole followed by a higher zero, well below the crossover, multiplies the low-frequency gain by the factor α and costs a few degrees of margin; with no pole at the origin, it reduces the step error but does not zero it.
    • Transfer function and factored formThe transfer function is a ratio of polynomials. Written in factored form, the DC gain stands alone in front, and each pole and zero marks a frequency on the Bode plot.
