Control basics

Start here

What the control basics section is for, and eight questions to find out, in a few minutes, which of its notes you need to read.

The converter and digital control notes use poles and zeros, Bode plots, the quality factor QQQ, phase margins and step responses without stopping to explain them. This section is the refresher on those ideas, for whoever got there and got stuck. The examples always come from the lab's converters, and every note ends with the way back to the point that called for it.

Nobody needs to read the whole section. Answer the questions below in your head, with paper and a calculator if you like, and only then open the answer. If you get one wrong, the answer says which note to read. If you get them all right, go back to the converter trails.

Whoever wants the full treatment, with the derivations, the filters and many more exercises, finds it in the reference book for this section, where the ideas of this section come from. The note Notation: from the reference course to this course translates the notation from one to the other.

4. Phase margin

The buck's loop crosses at 8 kHz with 41° of phase margin. How much more delay does it tolerate before oscillating?

Answer

41∘/(360∘×8 kHz)=14.241^\circ/(360^\circ \times 8\,\text{kHz}) = 14.241∘/(360∘×8kHz)=14.2 µs, almost three switching periods (2.8). If you got it wrong, read Feedback and margins.

5. Delay and pole

The firmware delays the response by (1+D) Ts(1 + D)\,T_s(1+D)Ts​. Why does this delay not show up as a pole in the s-plane? Why does it weigh more when the crossover goes up?

Answer

A delay has a magnitude of 1 at every frequency and a phase of −360∘f td-360^\circ f\,t_d−360∘ftd​, which grows without limit with frequency; a pole changes the magnitude, and its phase stops at −90°. At 8 kHz, the buck's delay takes 19.8°; at 18 kHz, 44.6°. If you got it wrong, read Feedback and margins.

6. From margin to step

A loop has 16° of phase margin. Do you expect a well-damped step?

Answer

No. By the rule ζ≈PM/100\zeta \approx \mathrm{PM}/100ζ≈PM/100, ζ≈0.16\zeta \approx 0.16ζ≈0.16, and the pair's overshoot is near 60 %, or more, ringing near the crossover. If you got it wrong, read From margin to damping: the ζ ≈ PM/100 rule and where it fails.

7. The step that starts backwards

In the boost, a step in the duty makes the output first fall a little and only then rise. What singularity explains this? Does it make the boost unstable?

Answer

A right-half-plane zero. No: stability depends only on the poles. The zero takes phase like a pole and limits the crossover frequency. If you got it wrong, read Right-half-plane zero.

8. The integrator

The buck's compensator has a pole at the origin, which takes 90° of phase at every frequency. What is it for?

Answer

To bring the steady-state error to zero: with the integrator, the loop gain at DC is infinite, and the error to a step, 1/(1+TL(0))1/(1 + T_L(0))1/(1+TL​(0)), goes to zero. The phase it takes is given back by the zeros. If you got it wrong, read PI, phase lead and the type III.
