Pole-zero cancellation and its risks

A compensator zero on a plant pole removes the pole from the loop gain, but not from the closed loop; an imperfect cancellation leaves a slow doublet, and a right-half-plane zero is never canceled.

First, see: Root locus (qualitative reading), PI, phase lead and the type III

Pole-zero cancellation puts a compensator zero on a plant pole, or a pole on a zero. In the loop gain, the two factors divide out, and the resonance disappears from the Bode plot. In the buck, the type III recipe puts its two zeros at the LC resonance (PI, phase lead and the type III), close to a cancellation.

What disappears and what stays

With G=NG/DGG = N_G/D_GG=NG​/DG​ and C=NC/DCC = N_C/D_CC=NC​/DC​, the closed-loop poles are the roots of

DC DG+NC NG=0.D_C\,D_G + N_C\,N_G = 0.DC​DG​+NC​NG​=0.

If NCN_CNC​ contains the factor of DGD_GDG​ with the LC pair, both terms contain it, and it is a root of the sum: the pair is still a closed-loop pole, in the same place and with the same damping. In the Root locus (qualitative reading), the branch that leaves the pole already starts at the zero that ends it, and it does not move with the gain.

The reference does not excite it, because the cancellation is on its path. A load or input step, which enters after the compensator, excites the pair, and the loop does not damp it.

Internal stability

Internal stability asks that every transfer function of the loop be stable: from the reference and from each disturbance to the output and to the duty cycle. A cancellation removes the pole from one of them, not from the others. That is why only a stable, well-damped pole is canceled.

The lab's 2p2z does not cancel the pair: its zeros sit at 2 kHz, 0.4 of the resonance. The 2p2z's slow doublet

The 2p2z has its own doublet. The integrator's branch goes from 0 toward the 2 kHz zeros and stops at −467 Hz, and each step sees it through the zeros of its own path:

  • Load step. The closed-loop output impedance has zeros at 0, from the integrator, and at R/(2πL)=289R/(2\pi L) = 289R/(2πL)=289 Hz, from the resistance of the inductor and the switch. The 467 Hz pole sits between them, almost canceled. At the instant of the step, the tail starts at only 15 mV per ampere, but it lasts: τ=0.34\tau = 0.34τ=0.34 ms. After one τ\tauτ, about 5.7 mV per ampere remain, the 5 to 6 mV of From margin to damping: the ζ ≈ MF/100 rule and where it fails.
  • Reference step. The nearest zeros are at 2 kHz, four times farther. The same pole carries 63 % of the step, and the output comes down slowly, with no overshoot (From margin to damping: the ζ ≈ MF/100 rule and where it fails).

The general rule: the distance to the zero sets the tail's amplitude; the pole's position sets its duration.

The operating point moves the target

In the buck, the pair's QQQ falls with the load: 7.1 at 9 Ω, 4.6 at 4.5 Ω. In the boost, the pair sits at D′/(2πLC)D'/(2\pi\sqrt{LC})D′/(2πLC​): at 1 A, 690 Hz at 12 V and 574 Hz at 10 V, 17 % lower, and QQQ falls from 4.4 to 3.7. A cancellation tuned at 12 V misses at 10 V by twice what it would with an inductor 20 % larger, which moves the pair 9 %. That is why the boost's 2p2z puts its zeros at 500 Hz, below the pair, without trying to cancel it (Robust design across the operating range).

What is never canceled

Canceling the boost's right-half-plane zero takes a compensator pole at +fRHP+f_{\mathrm{RHP}}+fRHP​, an unstable controller (Right-half-plane zero: the effect on the loop). Even a perfect cancellation breaks internal stability: the compensator's state grows as e2πfRHPte^{2\pi f_{\mathrm{RHP}} t}e2πfRHP​t, and the duty cycle runs to its limit. And it is never perfect: the zero moves from 14.1 kHz at 1 A to 6.9 kHz at 2 A. An unstable plant pole canceled by a compensator zero ends the same way: it stays in the response to disturbances and grows.

In zzz, the same test

Direct design in zzz writes the compensator as C=T/(G(1−T))C = T/(G(1 - T))C=T/(G(1−T)), which divides by the plant: each zero of GGG becomes a pole of CCC. The test is ∣z∣<1|z| < 1∣z∣<1 for each inverted zero. In the boost's sampled-data model, at 12 V and 2 A, the zero is at z=1.79z = 1.79z=1.79, outside the circle: it is not inverted.
