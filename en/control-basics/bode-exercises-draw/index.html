Exercise list: from the equation to the Bode plot

Six functions in factored form, in increasing difficulty, to draw the magnitude and phase asymptotes, with close corners, a differentiator's reference, RHP zeros, a Butterworth band-pass and a negative gain.

First, see: Bode by composition: from factored form to asymptote

Six exercises to practice the path from the equation to the plot, from the simplest to the hardest. In each one, draw the magnitude and phase asymptotes on paper: the corners, each segment's slope and level, and the points where the phase changes slope. Only then open the plot, with the "Show the plot" button, and the answer. The figure's switch turns on the exact curve, to check the error at the corners. In the answers, the slopes are in dB per decade and degrees per decade, and the "active lines" are the factors' phase lines in each interval.

Exercise

6. Negative gain. Draw the asymptotes of G(s)=−10 1+s/ω21+s/ω1G(s) = -10\,\dfrac{1 + s/\omega_2}{1 + s/\omega_1}G(s)=−101+s/ω1​1+s/ω2​​, with f1=100f_1 = 100f1​=100 Hz and f2=10f_2 = 10f2​=10 kHz. Where does the phase start?

Answer

Magnitude: 20 dB up to 100 Hz, −20 dB per decade up to 10 kHz, where it is −20 dB, and flat after. The phase starts at +180°, from the negative gain: −45 per decade from 10 Hz to 1 kHz, down to +90° (the pole), and +45 per decade from 1 kHz to 100 kHz, back to +180° (the zero). A program may show −180°: it is the same complex number.
