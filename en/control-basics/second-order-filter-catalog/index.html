Low-pass, high-pass and band-pass

In the low-pass and the high-pass (the inverted pair), the asymptotes meet at 0 dB, at f0f_0f0​, and the exact curve is QQQ: the pair's error, 20log⁡10Q20\log_{10} Q20log10​Q dB.

Band-reject or notch

The band-reject without a notch is the inverse of the band-pass, and it is improper: it has more zeros than poles, and ∣G∣→∞|G| \to \infty∣G∣→∞ at both ends. Its minimum is 0 dB at f0f_0f0​: the zeros sit off the jωj\omegajω axis. It appears as a factor of a product (the shape of an ideal PID); what rejects a band is the notch.

The notch puts the undamped zero pair at ±jω0\pm j\omega_0±jω0​: the magnitude is zero at f0f_0f0​. The two pairs' asymptotes cancel, and the asymptotic plot is 0 dB throughout. The dip shows only in the exact curve, and the phase steps from −90° to +90° at f0f_0f0​.

Resonator

The resonator is the inverse of the notch. Its poles sit on the jωj\omegajω axis: the gain at f0f_0f0​ is infinite, and the system on its own is marginally stable. The asymptotes cancel here too.

In the converters

The buck's output filter is a low-pass: with 22 µH, 47 µF and a 9 Ω load, f0=4.96f_0 = 4.96f0​=4.96 kHz. Without losses QQQ would be 13.2; with them, 7.1 (The buck's transfer function Gvd(s)G_{vd}(s)Gvd​(s)). A digital notch appears in Discretization table, in Tustin with prewarping. The resonator is the core of the proportional-resonant controller, which tracks a sinusoidal reference in an inverter.
