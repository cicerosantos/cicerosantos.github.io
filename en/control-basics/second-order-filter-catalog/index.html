Each filter is, then, a product of forms from Pole and zero at the origin: integrator and differentiator and Second-order forms: zeros, undamped, inverted and right-half-plane:

  • high-pass: the double differentiator (s/ω0)2(s/\omega_0)^2(s/ω0​)2 times the pole pair, which is the inverted pole pair;

  • notch: the undamped zero pair over the pole pair;
  • all-pass: the RHP zero pair over the pole pair.

In the lab, the buck's lossless LC pair would be Butterworth only with RC/L=0.707R\sqrt{C/L} = 0.707RC/L​=0.707, that is, a 0.48 Ω load, 19 A at 9 V: the plant is never flat, and it is the loop that damps it. The sensing filter in front of the ADC, on the other hand, is a design choice, and in its active version it is a Butterworth with n=2n = 2n=2 (Sensing and anti-aliasing filter).
