Second-order pole pair and quality factor Q

A pole pair is described by f₀ and Q. In the Bode plot, Q gives the height of the resonant peak; in the step, the balance between oscillation and decay, with the overshoot.

First, see: First-order pole and zero

The buck's LC filter stores energy in two elements, and the energy passes from one to the other: the response oscillates. A real first-order pole does not oscillate; for that you need a pair of complex poles.

Two numbers describe the pair:

  • f0f_0f0​, the frequency of the pair, the distance of the poles from the origin (in hertz, on the s/2πs/2\pis/2π plane);
  • QQQ, the quality factor, which says how lightly damped the pair is.

Where the poles are

The value of QQQ decides the shape of the roots:

  • Q<0.5Q < 0.5Q<0.5: two distinct real poles, no oscillation;
  • Q=0.5Q = 0.5Q=0.5: a double real pole at −ω0-\omega_0−ω0​;
  • Q>0.5Q > 0.5Q>0.5: a complex conjugate pair. The higher the QQQ, the closer to the jωj\omegajω axis;
  • Q→∞Q \to \inftyQ→∞: the poles lie on the axis, at ±jω0\pm j\omega_0±jω0​, and the response oscillates forever.

On the Bode plot

The asymptotes of the pair are two lines:

  • below f0f_0f0​, the magnitude stays at 0 dB (the DC gain) and the phase at 0°;
  • above f0f_0f0​, the magnitude falls 40 dB per decade and the phase tends to −180°.
