From Laplace to Bode

Unit 1 of classical control: from a Laplace expression to its asymptotic Bode plot and back, from the first-order forms to the second-order ones, the filters and two sets of exercises.

Start the trail →

This trail is unit 1 of classical control: all the first-order forms, then the second-order ones, their sum in any function, the filters and the Butterworth, and two sets of exercises. The forms and the exercises are generic, and the lab converters come in as examples. To know where to start, answer the questions in Start here first.

  1. Transfer function and factored form: the buck's plant as a ratio of polynomials, with the DC gain, the poles and the zeros in factored form.
  2. First-order pole and zero: the real pole and zero, each with its magnitude and phase asymptotes and the 3 dB error at the corner.
  3. Pole and zero at the origin: pure integrator and pure differentiator: the pure integrator and the pure differentiator, a ∓20 dB per decade line crossing 0 dB at fif_ifi​, with the phase constant at ∓90°.
  4. Right-half-plane pole and zero: the right-half-plane pole and zero, with the magnitude of the ordinary forms and the mirrored phase; the magnitude does not tell the root's half-plane, only the phase does.
  5. Inverted forms and the sign on the Bode plot: factors written in ω/s\omega/sω/s, which are 1 at high frequency, are a pure integrator or a pure differentiator times the ordinary form, and a minus sign adds ±180°.
  6. Second-order pole pair and quality factor Q: two poles together can oscillate, and the factor QQQ says how much; it is the buck's LC.
  7. Second-order forms: zeros, undamped, inverted and right-half-plane: the pair of zeros, the undamped pair, the inverted and right-half-plane pairs, each the ordinary pair with a mirror, a sign or a double pure integrator.
  8. Bode by composition: from factored form to asymptote: add the asymptotes of each factor to draw the Bode plot, and read the equation back from the curve; the table sums up all the forms.
  9. Second-order filters: each one a composition of the forms: the nine second-order filters, from low-pass to all-pass, each written as a product of the forms already seen.
  10. Butterworth: the Q of each section and the order n: the maximally flat filter as a cascade, with the QQQ of each section up to order 8 and a band-pass by composition.
  11. Exercise list: from the Bode plot to the equation: six plots to read the equation back from, from the gain and the corners to the QQQ and the right-half-plane roots.
  12. Exercise list: from the equation to the Bode plot: six equations to draw the asymptotes of, with close corners, RHP zeros, a Butterworth band-pass and a negative gain.

End of the trail

From here, go on to units 2 to 4, in the Closing and compensating the loop trail, which ends with the root locus. To see the same forms as circuits, follow the From circuit to transfer function trail: the RC and RL circuits, the ×10 probe and the Sallen-Key.
