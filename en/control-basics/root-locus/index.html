Root locus (qualitative reading)

How the closed-loop poles move as the compensator's gain rises, the rules for a hand sketch, and the reading in the buck and the boost, where the right-half-plane zero puts a ceiling on the gain.

First, see: Feedback and margins, Right-half-plane zero: the effect on the loop

The Bode plot reads the margin at one frequency. The root locus shows all the closed-loop poles and how they move as the compensator's gain rises. This course's designs are done with the Bode plot; the root locus is the s-plane view that completes it, optional for the converter.

The equation

The closed-loop poles are the roots of 1+T(s)=01 + T(s) = 01+T(s)=0 (Feedback and margins). With the compensator's gain KKK set apart:

1+K N(s)D(s)=0⟺D(s)+K N(s)=01 + K\,\frac{N(s)}{D(s)} = 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad D(s) + K\,N(s) = 01+KD(s)N(s)​=0⟺D(s)+KN(s)=0

With K=0K = 0K=0, the roots are the open-loop poles; with K→∞K \to \inftyK→∞, the zeros. Along the way, each root draws a branch, whose shape comes only from the angle condition, ∠N/D=±180∘\angle N/D = \pm 180^\circ∠N/D=±180∘.

The sketching rules

Write the factors as (s−pi)(s - p_i)(s−pi​) and (s−zj)(s - z_j)(s−zj​), with nnn poles, mmm zeros and K>0K > 0K>0:

  1. Branches. There are nnn, symmetric about the real axis.
  2. Start and end. They start at the poles and end at the finite zeros; the n−mn - mn−m left over go to infinity.
  3. Real axis. A point on the axis belongs to the locus when the number of real poles and zeros to its right is odd.
  4. Asymptotes. The branches that go to infinity follow lines at angles (2q+1) 180∘/(n−m)(2q + 1)\,180^\circ/(n - m)(2q+1)180∘/(n−m), q=0,1,…q = 0, 1, \ldotsq=0,1,…, which start from the centroid σa=(∑pi−∑zj)/(n−m)\sigma_a = \left(\sum p_i - \sum z_j\right)/(n - m)σa​=(∑pi​−∑zj​)/(n−m).
  5. Breakaway point. Two branches that meet on the real axis leave it as a complex pair; two complex branches return to the axis at a break-in point.
  6. The jωj\omegajω axis. The gain at which a branch crosses the axis is the critical gain, KcrK_{cr}Kcr​; the ratio Kcr/KK_{cr}/KKcr​/K is the gain margin, as a factor.

A pole further left is faster, and a pair on the line at angle θ\thetaθ from the negative real semi-axis has ζ=cos⁡θ\zeta = \cos\thetaζ=cosθ (Second-order pole pair and quality factor Q).

The right-half-plane zero flips the sign

The boost's zero is written 1−s/(2πfRHP)=−(s−2πfRHP)/(2πfRHP)1 - s/(2\pi f_{\mathrm{RHP}}) = -(s - 2\pi f_{\mathrm{RHP}})/(2\pi f_{\mathrm{RHP}})1−s/(2πfRHP​)=−(s−2πfRHP​)/(2πfRHP​), and the minus sign moves into the gain. With an odd number of such zeros, rule 3 asks for an even count, and the asymptotes leave at q⋅360∘/(n−m)q \cdot 360^\circ/(n - m)q⋅360∘/(n−m).

In the converters

The sketch uses the averaged model with the 2p2z's s-domain prototype (The 2p2z compensator), without the ESR zeros, far above the crossover; KKK is measured relative to the design. The design's poles and the critical gains with the delay come from the exact sampled-data model, in zzz.

Buck. There are four poles: the integrator at 0, the LC pair at −0.35±j4.95-0.35 \pm j4.95−0.35±j4.95 kHz, with 22 µH and 47 µF (ζ=0.071\zeta = 0.071ζ=0.071), and the compensator's pole at −60 kHz. There are two zeros, at −2 kHz. The integrator moves toward them and, at the design gain, stops at −467 Hz: it is the slow pole of From margin to damping: the ζ ≈ MF/100 rule and where it fails. The LC pair is pulled to the left, to −2.4±j7.2-2.4 \pm j7.2−2.4±j7.2 kHz (ζ=0.31\zeta = 0.31ζ=0.31).

Without delay, the two asymptotes are vertical, and the buck would be stable at any gain. The ceiling comes from the firmware's delay, (1+D) Ts=6.875(1 + D)\,T_s = 6.875(1+D)Ts​=6.875 µs at 200 kHz. It is neither a pole nor a zero, but its Padé approximation, (1−sτ/2)/(1+sτ/2)(1 - s\tau/2)/(1 + s\tau/2)(1−sτ/2)/(1+sτ/2), has a pole at −46.3 kHz and a right-half-plane zero at 1/(πτ)=46.31/(\pi\tau) = 46.31/(πτ)=46.3 kHz. With the delay, the pair bends to the right and crosses the axis (Padé: 5.8 K5.8\,K5.8K at 26 kHz; exact model: 5.1 K5.1\,K5.1K at 23 kHz, 14.1 dB, against 13.9 dB in the continuous Bode plot, a factor of 4.97).

Boost. At 12 V and 1 A, the poles are the integrator, the LC pair at −0.08±j0.69-0.08 \pm j0.69−0.08±j0.69 kHz and the pole at −30 kHz; the zeros, the compensator's two at −500 Hz and the zero at +14.1 kHz. With the even count, the segments from −500 Hz to 0 and from −30 kHz to −500 Hz, and the half-line right of +14.1 kHz, belong to the locus. The integrator goes toward −500 Hz and sits at −203 Hz at the design gain. The LC pair comes down to the axis, meets the branch coming from −30 kHz, and the two leave again as a pair. The only asymptote points to the right (n−m=1n - m = 1n−m=1, angle 0°): this pair crosses the jωj\omegajω axis and ends at the zero and at infinity on the right.

Exercise

Sketch the buck's locus without the delay, with the poles at 0, −0.35±j4.95-0.35 \pm j4.95−0.35±j4.95 kHz and −60 kHz, and the double zero at −2 kHz. How many asymptotes are there, at what angles, and where is the centroid? Can the loop become unstable?

Answer

n−m=4−2=2n - m = 4 - 2 = 2n−m=4−2=2 asymptotes, at ±90∘\pm 90^\circ±90∘. In hertz, σa=[0+2(−0.35)−60−2(−2)]/2=−28.4\sigma_a = [0 + 2(-0.35) - 60 - 2(-2)]/2 = -28.4σa​=[0+2(−0.35)−60−2(−2)]/2=−28.4 kHz. The asymptotes stay in the left half-plane, and the other branches end at −2 kHz: none crosses the jωj\omegajω axis. Without delay, the loop is stable at any gain.

Exercise

Applying rule 3 without flipping the sign, a colleague concludes that the boost's integrator runs from 0 to the zero at +14.1 kHz, and the loop would be unstable at any gain. Where is the mistake? What changes at 2 A?

Answer

The right-half-plane zero puts a minus sign in the gain: the right count is the even one, and the segment from 0 to +14.1 kHz, with one singularity to its right, is out. The integrator goes to the left. At 2 A, the zero drops to 6.9 kHz, and the critical gain falls from 7.9 K7.9\,K7.9K to 4.8 K4.8\,K4.8K (from 17.9 to 13.7 dB).
