Feedback and margins

How the loop closes, what the sensitivity 1/(1 + T) says, and why the phase and gain margins bound the peak of |S|.

First, see: Transfer function and factored form, Second-order pole pair and quality factor Q

On its own, the buck regulates poorly: a step in the duty cycle makes the output overshoot by 80 % of the jump and ring for 1.7 ms (Second-order pole pair and quality factor Q), and the gain changes with input and load. Feedback measures the output, compares it with the reference and corrects the duty cycle. One trip around the loop passes through the controller, the modulator, the plant and the sensor:

Crossover and margins

  • Crossover frequency fcf_cfc​: the last frequency at which ∣T∣|T|∣T∣ equals 1. A loop with an integrator can cross 1 more than once, and the margin is read at the last crossing. In order of magnitude, it is the loop's bandwidth.
  • Phase margin (MF): 180∘+∠T180^\circ + \angle T180∘+∠T at fcf_cfc​.

The MF measures how far TTT is from the point −1 at the crossover; the MG, at the point where the curve crosses the negative real axis. Read another way, the MF is the extra delay the loop tolerates, Δt=MF/(360∘fc)\Delta t = \mathrm{MF}/(360^\circ f_c)Δt=MF/(360∘fc​), and the MG is the extra gain it tolerates.

Stability of the closed loop

The loop is stable when 1+T(s)=01 + T(s) = 01+T(s)=0 has no roots in the right half-plane. This is the closed-loop criterion, and it does not reduce to the plant's poles. In open loop, stability depends only on the poles, as Transfer function and factored form showed; in closed loop, phase counts.

A right-half-plane zero of GvdG_{vd}Gvd​ creates no unstable pole, but it enters TTT as a phase lag, and that lag falls right near the crossover. The phase margin shrinks. With more gain, the crossover rises to where the zero takes away more phase, and the loop can oscillate. That is why Right-half-plane zero limits the crossover.

When the open-loop plant is stable, as the buck and the boost are in this course, the Nyquist criterion says the same: the curve of TTT must not wind around −1.

Exercise

A communication interruption starts delaying the duty-cycle write by a full period, 5 µs. What happens to the MF of the 2p2z? How many more periods could the loop take before it oscillates?

Answer

At 8 kHz, 5 µs costs 360∘⋅8000⋅5 µs=14.4∘360^\circ \cdot 8000 \cdot 5\,\text{µs} = 14.4^\circ360∘⋅8000⋅5µs=14.4∘: the MF drops from 41° to about 27°. The total extra delay tolerated is 14.2 µs, about 2.8 periods; with one period already lost, fewer than two remain.
