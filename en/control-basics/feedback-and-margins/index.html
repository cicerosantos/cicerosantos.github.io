Feedback and margins

Why close the loop, what the sensitivity 1/(1 + T_L) says and what the phase and gain margins measure.

First, see: Transfer function, poles and zeros, Second-order pair and quality factor Q

On its own, the buck regulates poorly: a step in the duty makes the output overshoot by 80 % of the jump and ring for 1.7 ms (Second-order pair and quality factor Q), and the gain changes with the input and the load. Feedback measures the output, compares it with the reference and corrects the duty. One trip around the loop goes through the controller, the plant and the sensor:

In this course's converters, the ADC reads the output voltage itself, and H=1H = 1H=1.

In the converters

In the buck, with the 2p2z, the loop crosses at 8 kHz with a PM of 41° and a GM of 13.9 dB (Loop gain, crossover frequency and margins). From the model, ∣TL∣=7.7|T_L| = 7.7∣TL​∣=7.7 at 100 Hz: a disturbance at that frequency reaches the output divided by 7.9 (∣S∣|S|∣S∣ = −17.9 dB). At 8 kHz, ∣S∣=1.42|S| = 1.42∣S∣=1.42 (+3 dB): there the loop amplifies. The worst point is 9.8 kHz, where TLT_LTL​ comes within 0.62 of the point −1-1−1.

Exercise

A communication interrupt starts delaying the duty's write by a whole period, 5 µs. What happens to the 2p2z's PM? How many more periods would the loop take before oscillating?

Answer

At 8 kHz, 5 µs cost 360∘⋅8000⋅5 µs=14.4∘360^\circ \cdot 8000 \cdot 5\,\text{µs} = 14.4^\circ360∘⋅8000⋅5µs=14.4∘: the PM falls from 41° to about 27°. The total extra delay tolerated is 14.2 µs, about 2.8 periods; with one period already lost, fewer than two are left.
