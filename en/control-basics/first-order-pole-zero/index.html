First-order pole and zero

A real pole brings the magnitude down at −20 dB per decade and lags the phase to −90°; a zero does the opposite. In time, the pole is an exponential with time constant 1/ωₚ.

First, see: Transfer function, poles and zeros

Almost every useful system is a product of first- and second-order blocks. This note covers the first-order ones: a real pole and a real zero, in standard form.

The pole

In the Bode plot, the pole reads as three straight lines, the asymptotes:

  • well below ωp\omega_pωp​, the magnitude stays at 0 dB and the phase at 0°;
  • well above, the magnitude falls 20 dB per decade (ten times smaller each decade) and the phase tends to −90°;
  • at ωp\omega_pωp​ itself, the real curve passes 3 dB below the asymptotes' corner, and the phase is exactly −45°.

The phase changes almost entirely within one decade each side: −5.7° at ωp/10\omega_p/10ωp​/10 and −84.3° at 10 ωp10\,\omega_p10ωp​.

−3 dB is not half

At ωp\omega_pωp​, the magnitude is 1/2=0.7071/\sqrt 2 = 0.7071/2​=0.707. Half, 0.5, is −6 dB, and happens at 3 ωp\sqrt 3\,\omega_p3​ωp​.

The zero

The zero, 1+s/ωz1 + s/\omega_z1+s/ωz​, mirrors the pole: the magnitude rises 20 dB per decade above ωz\omega_zωz​, and the phase leads up to +90°, passing +45° at ωz\omega_zωz​. Alone it is improper: the gain would grow without bound, and the response to a step would hold an impulse, because sss is a derivative. In a circuit, every zero comes with poles that limit the gain at high frequency. Together with a pole, (1+s/ωz)/(1+s/ωp)(1 + s/\omega_z)/(1 + s/\omega_p)(1+s/ωz​)/(1+s/ωp​), it becomes a buildable block, and that is how zeros appear in compensators (PI, phase lead and the type III).

The integrator

With the pole at the origin, G(s)=ωp/sG(s) = \omega_p/sG(s)=ωp​/s, there is no plateau: the magnitude falls 20 dB per decade at all frequencies, crosses 0 dB at ωp\omega_pωp​, and the phase stays at −90°. The step response is a ramp that never stops rising. It is a compensator's integral action, the one that brings the steady-state error to zero (PI, phase lead and the type III).

In the converters

The ESR zero is a real zero:

In the buck, with 47 µF and 5 mΩ, it sits at 677 kHz. At 8 kHz, the 2p2z's crossover, it adds 0.7° of phase; at 100 kHz, half the sampling frequency, 8.4°. In the boost and the buck-boost, with 100 µF and 10 mΩ, it sits at 159 kHz.

The buck's 2p2z has both kinds of block. The pole at 60 kHz takes only 0.08 dB and 7.6° at 8 kHz; its time constant is 2.65 µs, the TFT_FTF​ of the equivalent PID. Each of the two zeros at 2 kHz sits four times below 8 kHz, and gives +12.3 dB and +76° there.
