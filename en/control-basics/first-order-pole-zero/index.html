First-order pole and zero

A real pole drops the magnitude at −20 dB per decade and lags the phase down to −90°; a zero does the opposite. The asymptote is 3 dB off at the pole frequency, where the real phase is −45°.

First, see: Transfer function and factored form

The first-order factors are the blocks 1+s/ω1 + s/\omega1+s/ω of the factored form. This note covers the two simplest forms, the real pole and the real zero, both in the left half-plane, and how each becomes two straight lines on the Bode plot, one for the magnitude and one for the phase. The other first-order forms come in the next notes: at the origin (Pole and zero at the origin: pure integrator and pure differentiator), in the right half-plane (Right-half-plane pole and zero) and inverted (Inverted forms and the sign on the Bode plot).

On the Bode plot, a pole is drawn with two lines, the asymptotes: the magnitude stays at 0 dB up to the pole frequency, fpf_pfp​, and then falls 20 dB per decade; the phase stays at 0° up to fp/10f_p/10fp​/10, falls 45° per decade, and reaches −90° at 10fp10 f_p10fp​.

The real curve departs from the asymptotes only near the corner:

  • at fpf_pfp​, the magnitude is 1/21/\sqrt 21/2​, or −3.01 dB. The asymptote gives 0 dB there: the error is 3 dB;
  • one decade below or above, the error falls to 0.04 dB;
  • the real phase is exactly −45° at fpf_pfp​. The phase line also gives −45° at the corner, so it is right there. At the edges it is off by 5.7°: the real phase is −5.7° at fp/10f_p/10fp​/10 and −84.3° at 10fp10 f_p10fp​.

The zero

The zero, 1+s/ωz1 + s/\omega_z1+s/ωz​, sits at s=−ωzs = -\omega_zs=−ωz​ and is the mirror of the pole: the magnitude rises 20 dB per decade above fzf_zfz​, the phase rises from 0° at fz/10f_z/10fz​/10 to +90° at 10fz10 f_z10fz​, and the corner is also the point of +45° and 3 dB above the asymptote.

On its own, the zero is improper: the gain would grow without limit, and the step response would contain an impulse, because sss is a derivative. In a circuit, every zero comes with poles that limit the high-frequency gain. Together with a pole, (1+s/ωz)/(1+s/ωp)(1 + s/\omega_z)/(1 + s/\omega_p)(1+s/ωz​)/(1+s/ωp​), the pair is realizable.

The phase of each factor

Each factor has 0° of phase at the end of the frequency axis where it tends to 1, and the phase is continuous from there. For the pole and the zero, that end is DC: the phase starts at 0° and goes to ∓90°. In a product, the total phase is the sum of the factors' phases; a negative gain adds +180°. The next notes use the same rule.

In the converters

The ESR zero is a real zero, and it is the only first-order factor in the buck's plant:

fESR=12π RESR C(2)f_{\mathrm{ESR}} = \frac{1}{2\pi\,R_{\mathrm{ESR}}\,C}\tag{2}fESR​=2πRESR​C1​(2)
≡

In the buck, with 47 µF and 5 mΩ, it sits at 677 kHz. It adds 0.7° of phase at 8 kHz and 8.4° at 100 kHz, half the switching frequency. In the boost, with 100 µF and 10 mΩ, it sits at 159 kHz.
