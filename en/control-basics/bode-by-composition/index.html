Bode by composition: from factored form to asymptote

Draw a plant's Bode plot by hand, adding one factor at a time with the magnitude and phase asymptotes, and read the factored form back from the asymptotic Bode plot.

First, see: First-order pole and zero, Second-order filters: the catalogue

A plant in factored form is a product of factors. In dB, the magnitude of the product is the sum of the magnitudes, and in degrees, the phase is the sum of the phases. So the Bode plot of a plant is drawn by adding, one factor at a time, the asymptotes of each factor. This is the central skill of the unit: without it, the Bode plot is a curve with no explanation. All frequencies are in hertz, with ω=2πf\omega = 2\pi fω=2πf.

The phase line of each factor goes from 0° one decade below the corner to the full value one decade above it, on a logarithmic scale. It gives −45° at the corner of a real pole, and −90° at f0f_0f0​ for the pair. With a high QQQ, the real phase turns almost entirely near f0f_0f0​, and the two-decade line is off there (Second-order pole pair and quality factor Q).

The recipe

  1. Write the plant in factored form and read G0G_0G0​, and the level 20log⁡10G020\log_{10} G_020log10​G0​.
  2. List the corner frequencies in increasing order: fpf_pfp​ and fzf_zfz​ of each real factor, and f0f_0f0​ of each pair.
  3. Draw the low-frequency line, flat, at the level of G0G_0G0​.
  4. As you pass each corner, add the slope of that factor: −20 dB per decade for a real pole, +20 for a real zero, −40 for a pole pair. The slope of the curve is the sum of the slopes already passed.
  5. Draw the phase the same way: add the phase lines of the factors, each centred on its corner.
  6. Mark the error at the corner. For a real pole, the curve sits 3 dB below the asymptote; for a real zero, 3 dB above. For a pair, the curve sits 20log⁡10Q20\log_{10} Q20log10​Q dB above the asymptote: above if Q>1Q > 1Q>1, below if Q<1Q < 1Q<1.

The 3 dB error comes from ∣1+j∣=2|1 + j| = \sqrt 2∣1+j∣=2​: at the corner, the real factor is 1/21/\sqrt 21/2​ or 2\sqrt 22​. The pair's error comes from G(jω0)=−jQG(j\omega_0) = -jQG(jω0​)=−jQ: at the corner, the asymptote is 0 dB and the real curve is QQQ. Both are derived from the canonical form, not conventions.

Reading back: from the asymptote to the equation

The recipe runs in reverse, and it is the direction the student must master:

  1. the flat level at low frequencies gives 20log⁡10G020\log_{10} G_020log10​G0​;
  2. each change of slope marks a corner: −20 dB per decade, a real pole at that frequency; +20, a real zero; −40, a pole pair with f0f_0f0​ at that frequency;
  3. the QQQ of a pair comes from the real curve at f0f_0f0​: Q=10(L(f0)−20log⁡10G0)/20Q = 10^{(L(f_0) - 20\log_{10} G_0)/20}Q=10(L(f0​)−20log10​G0​)/20, with LLL the real magnitude in dB. This formula is exact only if the other factors are 1 at f0f_0f0​ (in the buck, the ESR zero adds 0.0002 dB there). If the real curve is not marked and QQQ is high, read it from the peak: Q≈10peak/20Q \approx 10^{\text{peak}/20}Q≈10peak/20, with an error of 3.3 % at Q=2Q = 2Q=2, 0.5 % at Q=5Q = 5Q=5 and 15 % at Q=1Q = 1Q=1. The peak does not separate a complex pair from two coincident real poles, because neither has a peak.

Exercise

A magnitude asymptote sits at 20 dB up to 1 kHz; it falls 20 dB per decade from 1 kHz to 10 kHz; and it falls 40 dB per decade from 10 kHz on. Write G(s)G(s)G(s) in factored form, ignoring the phase.

Answer

The 20 dB level gives G0=10G_0 = 10G0​=10. The slope of −20 dB per decade at 1 kHz is a real pole at 1 kHz; the extra −20 dB per decade at 10 kHz is a second real pole. So G(s)=10(1+s/(2π⋅1 kHz)) (1+s/(2π⋅10 kHz))G(s) = \dfrac{10}{(1 + s/(2\pi\cdot 1\,\text{kHz}))\,(1 + s/(2\pi\cdot 10\,\text{kHz}))}G(s)=(1+s/(2π⋅1kHz))(1+s/(2π⋅10kHz))10​. There is no complex pair: a pair would give a single corner, of −40 dB per decade, at f0f_0f0​, and here there are two −20 dB corners at different frequencies. Both poles are real and distinct.
