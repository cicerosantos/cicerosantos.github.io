Thermal design and part selection for the buck

From loss to junction temperature through the thermal resistance, with the iteration that couples RDS(on)R_{DS(on)}RDS(on)​ and temperature, and the criteria for choosing the MOSFETs, inductor and capacitors of the lab's buck.

First, see: Losses of the synchronous buck, RMS current in the buck's capacitors

The budget of Losses of the synchronous buck says how many watts each part dissipates. What remains is the temperature that takes it to, and whether each part handles the voltage, the current and the heat with margin. This procedure does both for the lab's buck.

1. From loss to temperature

Heat leaves the MOSFET's junction for the ambient through a chain of thermal resistances in series: junction to case (RθJCR_{\theta JC}RθJC​), case to heat sink (RθCSR_{\theta CS}RθCS​) and heat sink to air (RθSAR_{\theta SA}RθSA​). In steady state,

Tj=Ta+P (RθJC+RθCS+RθSA)T_j = T_a + P\,(R_{\theta JC} + R_{\theta CS} + R_{\theta SA})Tj​=Ta​+P(RθJC​+RθCS​+RθSA​)

In a surface-mount MOSFET, the "heat sink" is the board's copper. The data sheet then gives the total resistance, RθJAR_{\theta JA}RθJA​, measured on a standard board. The number only holds for that copper area.

Junction temperature

The junction temperature TjT_jTj​ is the temperature of the silicon. It is what the data sheet limits, not the case temperature. The thermal resistance RθR_\thetaRθ​, in °C/W, is the temperature rise per watt dissipated.

2. The iteration

RDS(on)R_{DS(on)}RDS(on)​ grows with temperature. More loss heats the junction, which raises RDS(on)R_{DS(on)}RDS(on)​, which raises the loss. The author's thermal model solves the coupling by fixed-point iteration:

  1. start with Tj=TaT_j = T_aTj​=Ta​;
  2. compute RDS(on)(Tj)R_{DS(on)}(T_j)RDS(on)​(Tj​) and the losses;
  3. compute the new TjT_jTj​ from the thermal network;
  4. repeat until the change falls below 0.001 °C.

For silicon MOSFETs, the model's law is RDS(on)(Tj)=RDS(on)(25 °C) [(Tj+273)/298]2R_{DS(on)}(T_j) = R_{DS(on)}(25\,°\text{C})\,[(T_j + 273)/298]^2RDS(on)​(Tj​)=RDS(on)​(25°C)[(Tj​+273)/298]2. When the data sheet has the curve of RDS(on)R_{DS(on)}RDS(on)​ against temperature, the exponent comes from it. If the loss is too large for the network, the iteration diverges: that is thermal runaway.

The inverse calculation says how much can be dissipated: Pmax⁡=(Tj,max⁡−Ta)/RθJAP_{\max} = (T_{j,\max} - T_a)/R_{\theta JA}Pmax​=(Tj,max​−Ta​)/RθJA​.

3. The selection criteria

MOSFET.

  • a rated VDSV_{DS}VDS​ of at least 1.2 Vin,max⁡1.2\,V_{in,\max}1.2Vin,max​, plus the switching voltage spike (Dead time and switching in the synchronous buck);
  • a continuous current above the peak Io+ΔiL/2I_o + \Delta i_L/2Io​+ΔiL​/2, at the working temperature;
  • the lowest RDS(on) QgR_{DS(on)}\,Q_gRDS(on)​Qg​ product: a low RDS(on)R_{DS(on)}RDS(on)​ cuts conduction, but a high QgQ_gQg​ raises the gate loss and slows switching.

Inductor. The inductance at the peak current with at least 20 % margin to saturation, or to the permeability drop in a powder core, and the temperature rise from copper and core losses (The buck inductor: core losses, Dowell and saturation).

Capacitors. A rated voltage of at least 1.2 times the maximum voltage across the capacitor, a rated RMS current above the one computed in RMS current in the buck's capacitors, and the capacitance checked at the working voltage, because a ceramic loses capacitance under DC bias.

In the converters

The lab's buck goes from 24 V to 9 V, up to 2 A. The ambient is assumed to be 40 °C.

MOSFETs. Both switches are Alpha & Omega's AO4262E (data sheet Rev.1.0, January 2016), 60 V, in an SO-8 package, with an RθJAR_{\theta JA}RθJA​ of 59 °C/W typical and 75 °C/W maximum in steady state, on 1 in² of 2 oz copper (RθJLR_{\theta JL}RθJL​ of 16 °C/W typical and 24 °C/W maximum to the lead), and Tj,max⁡T_{j,\max}Tj,max​ = 150 °C. The typical RDS(on)R_{DS(on)}RDS(on)​ goes from 5.2 mΩ at 25 °C to 8.3 mΩ at 125 °C, which gives an exponent of 1.62 in the law above; with 2, it would be 9.3 mΩ. The iteration, with 75 °C/W and the 2 A losses of Losses of the synchronous buck, converges in 3 steps:

Low side (conduction, dead time) 37.3 mW 42.8 °C 5.7 mΩ

The high side runs hotter: it is the one that turns on with loss, charges the node and recovers the low side's diode. With the upper bound of QrrQ_{rr}Qrr​, it would reach 464.6 mW and 74.9 °C. In QSPICE, with the VDMOS model, which has less CossC_{oss}Coss​, the high side dissipates 112.1 mW and the low side 33.1 mW through the drain: the order holds. The internal gate resistance (1.3 Ω, about 30 % of the gate energy with 3 Ω outside) adds 17.3 and 13.4 mW, 1 to 2 °C more; the budget counts that part in the gate, not in the switches. With TjT_jTj​ at 125 °C, each switch could dissipate (125−40)/75=1.13(125 - 40)/75 = 1.13(125−40)/75=1.13 W: the buck needs no heat sink, only copper on the board.

Voltage. 1.2⋅24=28.81.2\cdot 24 = 28.81.2⋅24=28.8 V. The author's transient model, with the default parasitic inductance of 17 nH in the power loop and tft_ftf​ = 7 ns, predicts di/dtdi/dtdi/dt = 0.38 A/ns at turn-off at 2.64 A and a spike of 6.4 V above the input: 30.4 V. A 40 V switch would keep less than 10 V of headroom; the 60 V one keeps almost 30 V. The peak current, 2.64 A, is far below the 13 A continuous the data sheet gives at 70 °C.

Why not the catalog's MOSFET. The lowest voltage in the author's component catalog is Infineon's IMLT40R011M2H, a 400 V SiC part with 11.3 mΩ, a QgQ_gQg​ of 85 nC and 18 V on the gate (data sheet values, extracted in the catalog). The RDS(on)R_{DS(on)}RDS(on)​ is twice as large, and the RDS(on) QgR_{DS(on)}\,Q_gRDS(on)​Qg​ product is 960 mΩ·nC, against 5.2⋅30=1565.2\cdot 30 = 1565.2⋅30=156 mΩ·nC. The gate alone would take 2⋅85 nC⋅18 V⋅200 kHz=0.612\cdot 85\,\text{nC}\cdot 18\,\text{V}\cdot 200\,\text{kHz} = 0.612⋅85nC⋅18V⋅200kHz=0.61 W, more than all the buck's estimated losses with the AO4262E, 0.58 W.

Inductor. The powdered-iron core of The buck inductor: core losses, Dowell and saturation (T33/19/11, 45 turns) shows no permeability drop up to the 2.64 A peak. It loses 220.9 mW, copper and core together. The catalog's surface thermal model gives 51.7 °C/W for this toroid: a rise of 11.4 °C.

Capacitors. At the input, a rated voltage of 35 V or more, an RMS current of 1.02 A (worst case, 18 V and 2 A) and at least 13.9 µF effective for 1 % ripple at 18 V (0.18 V). At the 9 V output, 16 V is enough, with 0.37 A RMS.
