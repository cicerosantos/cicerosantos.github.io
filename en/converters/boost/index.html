Synchronous boost 12 V → 48 V: worked example

The lab's boost, from the specifications to the firmware, with the numbers of every step, the right-half-plane zero that holds the crossover at 1.5 kHz and the fifteen scenarios simulated in QSPICE.

This is the converter of the From the boost to the digital loop trail: a synchronous boost controlled by a microcontroller, simulated in QSPICE with the same C firmware as the Synchronous buck 24 V → 9 V: worked example. Each step below points to the note that explains the theory and shows the number it gives for this boost.

Specifications

Quantity Value

Duty ratio from 0 to 0.85

Design summary. Voltage-mode control, with one ADC sample and one controller update per period, as in the buck. The default compensator is a 2p2z with crossover at 1.5 kHz, two zeros at 500 Hz (below the resonance, at 690 Hz) and a pole at 30 kHz. The crossover is low because of the right-half-plane zero, which comes down to 6.9 kHz at 2 A. The numbers below come from the validated model, at 12 V, at 1 A and at 2 A.

  4. Transfer function Gvd(s)G_{vd}(s)Gvd​(s) of the boost. DC gain of 190 (45.6 dB), about V/D′V/D'V/D′; resonance at 690 Hz, with QQQ = 4.4 at 1 A and 3.6 at 2 A; at 1.5 kHz, the plant is 34.1 dB.
  5. The right-half-plane zero. The right-half-plane zero lies at 14.1 kHz at 1 A and 6.9 kHz at 2 A. At 1.5 kHz, it takes 6.1° and 12.3° of phase. The ESR zero (The ESR zero) lies at 159 kHz and gives only 0.5°.
  6. Sampling instant and the ESR in the boost. The sample, just before G1 turns on, sits about 50 mV above the average: with the samples at 48 V, the average is 47.95 V. In the plant the ADC sees, the right-half-plane zero rises to 22.1 kHz at 1 A and 8.4 kHz at 2 A, and the margin gains 1.6°.
  7. Loop gain, crossover frequency and margins. With the 2p2z, the crossover is at 1.5 kHz, with a phase margin of 45.3° and a gain margin of 17.5 dB at 1 A (the phase crosses −180° at 8.4 kHz); at 2 A, 40.6° and 13.4 dB (6.1 kHz).
  8. Computation and PWM delay.   9. Robust design across the operating range. The 1.5 kHz crossover sits at fRHP/4.6f_{\mathrm{RHP}}/4.6fRHP​/4.6 at 2 A. With the input at 10 V and 2 A, the zero comes down to 4.7 kHz, the crossover to 1.27 kHz, and the margin to 34.7°.

  13. DPWM resolution and limit cycles. With a 170 MHz timer, a period has 1700 counts (10.7 bits). One count moves the output 190/1700=0.11190/1700 = 0.11190/1700=0.11 V, 7.6 ADC steps: the DPWM is much coarser than the ADC.
  14. Q15 fixed point. b0b_0b0​, b1b_1b1​, b2b_2b2​ and a2a_2a2​ fit in Q15; a1=−1.15a_1 = -1.15a1​=−1.15 does not, as in the buck. The PID in velocity form has a1=−1.443a_1 = -1.443a1​=−1.443.
  15. Anti-windup and duty saturation. The duty goes from 0 to 0.85. On the reference steps without a slew, the derivative kick takes the duty to one of the limits, and the integrator clamp and the velocity form behave very differently.
  16. The 2p2z in C, in the interrupt. At 100 kHz, the interrupt has 10 µs, or 1700 cycles of a 170 MHz core.

The PID has the same zeros as the 2p2z and the filter's pole at 20 kHz instead of 30 kHz; it loses 3° of margin. The aggressive design puts the zeros on the resonance and crosses at 4 kHz, where the 2 A zero already takes 30°. The unstable one crosses above the 2 A zero.

Load step from 1 to 2 A: margins and the right-half-plane zero

The step takes the converter to the point of lowest zero (Loop gain, crossover frequency and margins, The right-half-plane zero).

∿Open in the oscilloscope: PID: the output falls to 46.86 V and comes back with a small overshoot; at 2 A, the margin is 38°.

∿Open in the oscilloscope: 2p2z: the output falls to 46.89 V, the 1.06 V predicted from the capacitor and the crossover.

∿Open in the oscilloscope: Aggressive: it sags less, to 47.13 V, but with 0.5° of margin at 2 A it rings near 4.3 kHz for milliseconds.

∿Open in the oscilloscope: Unstable: the oscillation grows after the step, and the output's average collapses.

Reference ramp from 48 to 40 V: the loop following a slow reference

At 10 V/ms, the ramp lasts 0.8 ms and, in the stable designs, nothing saturates (Anti-windup and duty saturation, section 5).

∿Open in the oscilloscope: PID: the output follows the ramp down to 40 V.

∿Open in the oscilloscope: 2p2z: the same ramp, without going past 40 V.

∿Open in the oscilloscope: Aggressive: faster, it goes past the reference to 39.9 V.

∿Open in the oscilloscope: Unstable: before the step, the duty hits 0.85, and the output already oscillates, with a period of 0.13 to 0.16 ms (6 to 8 kHz), 0.7 V peak to peak and an average of 45.1 V, below the 48 V.

Input from 12 to 10 V: the plant that changes

The input drop lowers D′D'D′, the resonance and the zero (Robust design across the operating range).

∿Open in the oscilloscope: PID: the output sags to 45.29 V and comes back in about 1 ms.

∿Open in the oscilloscope: 2p2z: down to 45.32 V, and back in the ±2 % band in 0.96 ms.

∿Open in the oscilloscope: Aggressive: with the higher crossover, only down to 47.31 V; at 1 A it still has 17.7° of margin at 10 V.

∿Open in the oscilloscope: Unstable: the oscillation goes on with the new input.

Steps without a slew: saturation and windup

The derivative kick saturates the duty, and the firmware's form decides what comes next (Anti-windup and duty saturation).

∿Open in the oscilloscope: Reference from 48 to 40 V without a slew: one period with zero duty, and the inductor current reaches −16 A.

∿Open in the oscilloscope: Reference from 40 to 48 V without a slew, PID with the integrator clamp: four periods at 0.85 and settling in 1.0 ms, without overshoot.

∿Open in the oscilloscope: The same step with the PID in velocity form: the duty collapses, the current goes to −34.7 A and the output to 7.7 V.

In the converters

Compared with the Synchronous buck 24 V → 9 V: worked example, this boost crosses five times lower (1.5 against 8 kHz) at half the switching frequency, and that is why it needs a 100 µF capacitor to sag 1 V on a 1 A step. Exercise

With the crossover at 1.5 kHz, how much phase do the right-half-plane zero and the delay take together at 2 A? And if the crossover were at 4 kHz?

Answer

At 1.5 kHz: arctan⁡(1.5/6.87)=12.3∘\arctan(1.5/6.87) = 12.3^\circarctan(1.5/6.87)=12.3∘ from the zero and 360∘⋅1500⋅14.5 µs=7.8∘360^\circ \cdot 1500 \cdot 14.5\,\text{µs} = 7.8^\circ360∘⋅1500⋅14.5µs=7.8∘ from the delay, 20.1° in total. At 4 kHz: 30.2° and 20.9°, 51° in total, which add to the plant's roughly −180° above the resonance. That is what brings the aggressive design down to 0.5° of margin at 2 A.

Exercise

Why does the PID with the integrator clamp get through the step from 40 to 48 V without a slew, and the same PID in velocity form does not?

Answer

Both saturate at 0.85 in the first period, from the derivative kick. The clamp keeps the integrator at a reasonable value, and the duty comes back smoothly. In velocity form, the next period's b1 e[k−1]b_1\,e[k-1]b1​e[k−1] term takes back the whole kick, which was never applied, and the duty falls to 0.09 and stays at zero for two periods: the current reverses down to −34.7 A, and the output collapses to 7.7 V.
