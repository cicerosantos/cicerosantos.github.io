Losses of the synchronous buck

The conduction, switching, dead-time, gate, inductor and capacitor losses of the lab's buck, added up into a budget and an efficiency-versus-load curve.

First, see: Duty ratio and ripple of the buck in CCM, RMS current in the buck's capacitors

The buck's averaged model treats the losses as one series resistance, Rs=RL+RonR_s = R_L + R_{on}Rs​=RL​+Ron​ (The averaged switch model). That is enough for the loop, but not to say how hot the converter gets. This note splits the losses by mechanism, with the formulas the author's synchronous buck loss model uses, and adds everything up for the lab's buck.

The mechanisms

Conduction in the switches. The high-side switch conducts during DDD, the low-side one during 1−D1 - D1−D. With IL,rms=Io2+ΔiL2/12I_{L,\mathrm{rms}} = \sqrt{I_o^2 + \Delta i_L^2/12}IL,rms​=Io2​+ΔiL2​/12​,

Pcond=IL,rms2 D Ron+IL,rms2 (1−D) RonP_{\mathrm{cond}} = I_{L,\mathrm{rms}}^2\,D\,R_{on} + I_{L,\mathrm{rms}}^2\,(1 - D)\,R_{on}Pcond​=IL,rms2​DRon​+IL,rms2​(1−D)Ron​

High-side switching. When it switches, the high-side switch has voltage and current at the same time. In the linear-overlap model, it turns on at the valley current and turns off at the peak:

Psw=12 Vin (Ivalley tr+Ipeak tf) fsP_{\mathrm{sw}} = \tfrac12\,V_{in}\,(I_{\mathrm{valley}}\,t_r + I_{\mathrm{peak}}\,t_f)\,f_sPsw​=21​Vin​(Ivalley​tr​+Ipeak​tf​)fs​

The low-side switch turns on with its body diode already conducting, at almost zero voltage, and has no such loss (Dead time and switching in the synchronous buck).

Output capacitance and reverse recovery. Every period, the high-side switch charges the node's capacitance and removes the charge stored in the low-side switch's diode; both losses land in it:

PCoss≈Qoss Vin fs,PQrr=Qrr Vin fsP_{C_{oss}} \approx Q_{oss}\,V_{in}\,f_s, \qquad P_{Q_{rr}} = Q_{rr}\,V_{in}\,f_sPCoss​​≈Qoss​Vin​fs​,PQrr​​=Qrr​Vin​fs​

QossQ_{oss}Qoss​ is the switch's output charge at the working voltage; the form 12 Coss Vin2 fs\tfrac12\,C_{oss}\,V_{in}^2\,f_s21​Coss​Vin2​fs​ underestimates twice over: it counts only the switch's own energy, not the charge of the other one (with constant CCC, QVfs=CV2fsQ V f_s = C V^2 f_sQVfs​=CV2fs​), and it uses the CossC_{oss}Coss​ at 30 V, smaller than at low voltages: below, 30.0 against 105.6 mW. QrrQ_{rr}Qrr​ grows with the current the diode was carrying.

Dead time. In the two dead times of each period, the current flows through the body diode, with a drop VSDV_{SD}VSD​ much larger than I RonI\,R_{on}IRon​:

Pdt=VSD (Ivalley+Ipeak) tdt fsP_{dt} = V_{SD}\,(I_{\mathrm{valley}} + I_{\mathrm{peak}})\,t_{dt}\,f_sPdt​=VSD​(Ivalley​+Ipeak​)tdt​fs​

Gate drive. The driver charges and discharges each switch's gate once per period: Pg=2 Qg VGS fsP_g = 2\,Q_g\,V_{GS}\,f_sPg​=2Qg​VGS​fs​. It goes mostly to the driver and the gate resistors; the part in the switch's internal resistance heats the package.

Inductor and capacitors. The inductor's copper dissipates IL,rms2RLI_{L,\mathrm{rms}}^2 R_LIL,rms2​RL​, the core loses a power that depends only on the ripple (The buck inductor: core losses, Dowell and saturation), and each capacitor dissipates Irms2RESRI_{\mathrm{rms}}^2 R_{\mathrm{ESR}}Irms2​RESR​ (RMS current in the buck's capacitors).

The efficiency is η=Po/(Po+∑P)\eta = P_o/(P_o + \sum P)η=Po​/(Po​+∑P).

Exercise

If the dead time went from 50 ns to 100 ns, how much would the diode loss and the efficiency change at 2 A?

Answer

The extra 50 ns are all diode time, at both edges: (0.71⋅1.36+0.73⋅2.64)⋅50 ns⋅200 kHz=29.0(0.71\cdot 1.36 + 0.73\cdot 2.64)\cdot 50\,\text{ns}\cdot 200\,\text{kHz} = 29.0(0.71⋅1.36+0.73⋅2.64)⋅50ns⋅200kHz=29.0 mW more, from 22.6 to 51.6 mW. The total goes from 580.6 to 609.7 mW, and η=18/(18+0.6097)=96.72\eta = 18/(18 + 0.6097) = 96.72η=18/(18+0.6097)=96.72 %, against 96.88 %.

Exercise

Does driving only the low-side switch at 4.5 V instead of 10 V pay off? Use QgQ_gQg​ = 15 nC and a typical RDS(on)R_{DS(on)}RDS(on)​ of 6.6 mΩ at 4.5 V (against 5.2 mΩ at 10 V), at 2 A.

Answer

Low-side gate: from 30 nC⋅10 V⋅200 kHz=60.030\,\text{nC}\cdot 10\,\text{V}\cdot 200\,\text{kHz} = 60.030nC⋅10V⋅200kHz=60.0 mW to 15⋅4.5⋅0.2=13.515\cdot 4.5\cdot 0.2 = 13.515⋅4.5⋅0.2=13.5 mW, 46.5 mW less. Low-side conduction: 13.4⋅6.6/5.2=17.113.4\cdot 6.6/5.2 = 17.113.4⋅6.6/5.2=17.1 mW, 3.6 mW more. The gain is 42.9 mW at 2 A and 45.5 mW at 1 A. The low-side switch turns on at zero voltage, so the lower gate voltage does not weigh on switching. The high-side one does: the driver's current on the Miller plateau falls with VGSV_{GS}VGS​, the turn-on would be several times slower, and the conclusion reverses.
