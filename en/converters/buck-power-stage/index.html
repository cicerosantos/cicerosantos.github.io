Buck power stage

From the sized filter to the parts: the RMS current in the capacitors, the losses, the dead time, the inductor and the thermal design of the lab's buck.

Start the trail →

This trail takes the buck from the sized filter to the chosen parts: how much current the capacitors carry, where power is lost, what happens in the dead time, how much the inductor loses in core and copper and when it saturates, and what temperature all that leads to. It starts from the first two steps of the From the analog buck to the digital loop trail, Duty ratio and ripple of the buck in CCM and Sizing L and C. The worked example is the Synchronous buck 24 V → 9 V: worked example.

  1. RMS current in the buck's capacitors: the input capacitor carries a pulsed current, much larger than the output capacitor's, which sees only the inductor's ripple.
  2. Losses of the synchronous buck: the conduction, switching, dead-time, gate, inductor and capacitor losses, added up into a budget and an efficiency curve.
  3. Dead time and switching in the synchronous buck: the switch node during the dead time, why the high-side switch switches with loss and the low-side one does not, and how to choose the dead time.
  4. The buck inductor: core losses, Dowell and saturation: the ripple becomes core loss, copper loses more at high frequency, and saturation is checked against the peak current.
  5. Thermal design and part selection for the buck: from loss to junction temperature, and the criteria for choosing the MOSFETs, inductor and capacitors.

End of the trail

With the parts chosen, the next step is the loop: follow the From the analog buck to the digital loop trail from the averaged model, The averaged switch model.
