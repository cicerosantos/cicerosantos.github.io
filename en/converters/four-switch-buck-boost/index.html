Four-switch buck-boost

Two legs and one inductor that step the voltage down or up; the buck, boost and buck-boost modes, the plant that changes with the input and the mode the lab uses.

First, see: Transfer function Gvd(s)G_{vd}(s)Gvd​(s) of the boost

An 8 to 16 V battery feeding a 12 V bus must step the voltage down and up. The four-switch buck-boost does both with a single inductor, without inverting the output: a buck leg at the input (Q1 on top, Q2 below, node A) and a boost leg at the output (Q3 on top, Q4 below, node B), with the inductor between the two nodes.

Three modes

  • Buck. Q3 stays on and Q4 off; only the input leg switches. It is a buck: V=D VinV = D\,V_{in}V=DVin​, the inductor current is the load's, and the plant has no right-half-plane zero.
  • Boost. Q1 stays on and Q2 off; only the output leg switches. It is a boost: V=Vin/(1−D)V = V_{in}/(1 - D)V=Vin​/(1−D), with the right-half-plane zero.
  • Buck-boost. Both legs switch together: G1 turns on Q1 and Q4, and the inductor receives the input; G2 turns on Q2 and Q3, and the inductor discharges into the output. From the volt-second balance, D Vin=D′ VD\,V_{in} = D'\,VDVin​=D′V, that is, V/Vin=D/D′V/V_{in} = D/D'V/Vin​=D/D′.

The first two modes have lower losses, but the firmware must decide which mode it is in and switch modes when VinV_{in}Vin​ crosses VVV, where each one's duty reaches its limit. The third covers the whole range with a single duty, without transitions. The price is that all four switches commutate every period and the inductor carries the input and output currents added together, I=Io/D′I = I_o/D'I=Io​/D′.

The lab's mode

The lab's converter always runs in buck-boost mode: G1 drives Q1 and Q4, G2 drives Q2 and Q3. The simulated firmware is the same as the buck's and the boost's, in floating point, with one duty and one voltage loop; the Q15 version was not simulated. The small-signal model and every number on this site are for that mode.

The plant changes with the input

With the output fixed, the input sets DDD: from V/(Vin+V)V/(V_{in} + V)V/(Vin​+V), 0.57 at 9 V to 0.44 at 15 V, without losses. Hence:

  • The resonance rises with D′D'D′, about 30 % from 9 to 15 V.
  • The DC gain hardly changes: without losses, Gvd(0)=(Vin+V)/D′G_{vd}(0) = (V_{in} + V)/D'Gvd​(0)=(Vin​+V)/D′, and both quantities grow together.
  • The right-half-plane zero comes down when the input falls and when the load rises. The worst case is the minimum input with the maximum load.

A fixed compensator must work at all these points; how to choose it is in Robust design across the operating range.
