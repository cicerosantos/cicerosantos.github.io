The buck inductor: core losses, Dowell and saturation

How the current ripple becomes core loss through the iGSE, why copper loses more at high frequency than its DC resistance suggests, and how to check saturation, with three designs of the lab's 22 µH inductor.

First, see: Sizing L and C, Losses of the synchronous buck

The lab model reduces the inductor to an inductance and a resistance, RLR_LRL​. A real inductor has a core that loses energy every flux cycle, a winding whose resistance rises with frequency, and a current above which the inductance drops. This note handles the three effects with the magnetics functions of the author's catalog.

The flux in the core

With NNN turns on a core of effective area AeA_eAe​, Faraday's law gives vL=NAe dB/dtv_L = N A_e\,dB/dtvL​=NAe​dB/dt. Since vL=L diL/dtv_L = L\,di_L/dtvL​=LdiL​/dt, the flux swing follows the current swing:

ΔB=L ΔiLNAe,Bpeak=L IpeakNAe\Delta B = \frac{L\,\Delta i_L}{N A_e}, \qquad B_{\mathrm{peak}} = \frac{L\,I_{\mathrm{peak}}}{N A_e}ΔB=NAe​LΔiL​​,Bpeak​=NAe​LIpeak​​

The DC part of BBB spends no energy in the core, but brings it closer to saturation. The AC part, ΔB\Delta BΔB, is the one that loses.

Core losses

The Steinmetz equation describes a material's loss per volume under sinusoidal flux: Pv=k fα BpeakβP_v = k\,f^{\alpha}\,B_{\mathrm{peak}}^{\beta}Pv​=kfαBpeakβ​, with kkk, α\alphaα and β\betaβ fitted to the manufacturer's curves. The buck's flux is triangular: it rises during DTsDT_sDTs​ and falls during (1−D) Ts(1 - D)\,T_s(1−D)Ts​. The improved generalized Steinmetz equation (iGSE) uses the rate dB/dtdB/dtdB/dt of each segment and, for the triangular wave, becomes

Pv=ki fα ΔBβ [D1−α+(1−D)1−α]P_v = k_i\,f^{\alpha}\,\Delta B^{\beta}\,\left[D^{1-\alpha} + (1 - D)^{1-\alpha}\right]Pv​=ki​fαΔBβ[D1−α+(1−D)1−α]

where kik_iki​ follows from kkk, α\alphaα and β\betaβ, with no new measurement. The loss is PvP_vPv​ times the effective volume VeV_eVe​. With α\alphaα near 1, as in powdered irons, the waveform correction almost vanishes; it weighs more in materials with a larger α\alphaα and a duty ratio near 0 or 1.

Winding losses

At high frequency, the current crowds near the conductor's surface, within a depth

δ=ρπfμ0\delta = \sqrt{\frac{\rho}{\pi f \mu_0}}δ=πfμ0​ρ​​

Saturation

In a gapped ferrite core, the inductance is nearly constant until BpeakB_{\mathrm{peak}}Bpeak​ approaches BsatB_{\mathrm{sat}}Bsat​, and then it collapses. In a powder core, the gap is distributed, and the permeability falls gradually with the magnetic field, NI/leN I/l_eNI/le​. In both cases, the check is made at the peak current and at the working temperature: BsatB_{\mathrm{sat}}Bsat​ falls with temperature.

In the converters

The lab's buck has L = 22 µH and RLR_LRL​ = 30 mΩ, with no core loss, at 200 kHz. The three designs below use Magmattec powdered-iron toroids from the author's catalog (geometry, ALA_LAL​, and the Steinmetz coefficients and permeability roll-off fitted to the curves in the data sheets of materials 034 and 002), round wire, and the winding at 60 °C, where δ\deltaδ = 0.159 mm. Two approximations in the copper pull in opposite directions. Dowell's FRF_RFR​ above does not use the 0.886 factor for round wire nor the layer's porosity, which puts it about 11 % high. The catalog's mean turn length, 35.4 mm, is below the perimeter of the T33/19/11's cross-section, 36 mm; with the 1 mm wire, it would be about 39 mm, and RdcR_{dc}Rdc​ is about 10 % low.

  • The material decides the core. The 034 gives the lab's RLR_LRL​ with few turns, but at 200 kHz it loses almost half a watt in the core, more than all the copper. The 002 is made for this frequency: with less permeability, it needs more turns, which also reduce ΔB\Delta BΔB, and it loses 19 times less. With α\alphaα = 1.04, the iGSE stays within 0.5 % of the sinusoidal Steinmetz.
  • The second layer is expensive. On the T26/14/11, the 41 turns do not fit in one layer: FRF_RFR​ goes from 6.3 to 18.8, and the AC part of the copper loss reaches 92.4 mW, against 34.7 mW in the single-layer design. The larger toroid, T33/19/11, fits everything in one layer and is the chosen design; stranded insulated wire is the other way out.
  • Saturation. In the 034, the permeability falls to 98.7 % at 2 A DC (22.7 µH) and only reaches 80 % at 13.4 A. In the 002, it does not fall up to the 2.64 A peak, and BpeakB_{\mathrm{peak}}Bpeak​ = 18.5 mT against a BsatB_{\mathrm{sat}}Bsat​ of 450 mT at 100 °C.

The chosen design has 22.3 µH with no current and 35 mΩ, a little above the lab's RLR_LRL​. Thermal design and part selection for the buck computes its temperature rise, and Losses of the synchronous buck uses its core loss.
