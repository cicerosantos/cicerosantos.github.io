Transfer function Gvd(s)G_{vd}(s)Gvd​(s) of the boost

The boost's plant in CCM has a DC gain of about V/D', a pair of poles that comes down with D' and a right-half-plane zero; the ADC also sees an ESR term and the firmware's delay.

First, see: The averaged switch model

In the buck, the duty only multiplies the input, and the plant is VinV_{in}Vin​ times the LC filter (The buck's transfer function Gvd(s)G_{vd}(s)Gvd​(s)). In the boost, the averaged cell multiplies the state variables: the switch node is d′ vd'\,vd′v and the current delivered to the output is d′ iLd'\,i_Ld′iL​, with d′=1−dd' = 1 - dd′=1−d (The averaged switch model). The plant inherits three differences from this: the gain depends on D′D'D′, so does the resonance, and a right-half-plane zero appears.

The plant the ADC sees

The controller does not see GvdG_{vd}Gvd​. The sample is taken just before G1 turns on and also reads an ESR drop the average does not have (Sampling instant and the ESR in the boost), and the computed duty only applies in the next period (Computation and PWM delay). To first order, the plant the controller sees is GvdG_{vd}Gvd​ plus the ESR term, times a delay of (1+D/2) Ts(1 + D/2)\,T_s(1+D/2)Ts​. The site uses the sampled-data model of the switched circuit, with no fitted factor, and it was checked in QSPICE by adding a small sine to the duty in the firmware (Measuring the loop gain by injecting into the duty): at 1 and 2 A (48 and 24 Ω), it stayed within 0.2 dB and 1.4° of the switched circuit from 300 Hz to 7 kHz, across the resonance.

Doubling the load hardly changes the gain and the resonance, but halves the zero. Lowering the input from 12 to 10 V moves everything: D′D'D′ falls from 0.246 to 0.203, the resonance comes down 17 %, the gain rises 1.5 dB and the zero comes down by another third. At 1.5 kHz, the 2p2z's crossover, the plant is 34.1 dB at 1 A: that is what the compensator must attenuate there. The full example is in Synchronous boost 12 V → 48 V: worked example.
