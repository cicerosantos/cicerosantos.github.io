The averaged switch model

Replacing the switch pair by its average over one period turns the buck into a continuous circuit; perturbed and linearized, it gives the small-signal model.

First, see: Duty ratio and ripple of the buck in CCM

To design the control we need a transfer function, but the switch turns on and off every TsT_sTs​ and has none. The way out is to look at the average of each quantity over one period, which varies slowly. This note does the derivation for the buck; the result is the plant of the next note.

Where averaging stops being valid

The averaged model assumes two things, and each has a limit.

  1. Small ripple. The average of a product is only the product of the averages if ΔiL\Delta i_LΔiL​ and Δvo\Delta v_oΔvo​ are small next to the averages. The model does not see the ripple, but the ADC does: it samples at a fixed point of the waveform and reads a constant offset (see Duty ratio and ripple of the buck in CCM).
  2. Slow variations. The average only describes perturbations much slower than the switching. A perturbation near fs/2f_s/2fs​/2 creates, at the switching node, a component at fs−ff_s - ffs​−f of almost the same frequency, which the filter lets through and which is confused with it by sampling. In practice, the model holds up to a fraction of fs/2f_s/2fs​/2, and the loop's crossover stays well below that.

The model also does not know the control is digital: the sample, the computation and the PWM delay the response by (1+D)Ts(1 + D)T_s(1+D)Ts​, the computation delay, which enters as a factor e−s(1+D)Tse^{-s(1+D)T_s}e−s(1+D)Ts​ multiplying the plant.

DCM and diode emulation

In DCM, the current rests at zero for part of the period and the averaged cell changes: the plant loses one of the LC poles. The synchronous buck stays in forced CCM and does not have this problem, unless the firmware turns the low-side switch off on negative current.

In the converters

In the lab's buck (24 V → 9 V, 200 kHz), RsR_sRs​ = 30 mΩ + 10 mΩ = 40 mΩ, and at 1 A the duty ratio with losses is (9+0.04)/24=0.377(9 + 0.04)/24 = 0.377(9+0.04)/24=0.377, against 0.375 ideal. The averaged model with the (1+D)Ts(1+D)T_s(1+D)Ts​ delay was checked in QSPICE by adding a small sine to the duty inside the firmware: it stayed within 0.3 dB and 0.7° of the switched circuit from 500 Hz to 15 kHz, across the resonance. At 30 kHz, already almost a third of fs/2f_s/2fs​/2 = 100 kHz, the magnitude still matches, but the phase is 3.9° off and the point fails the linearity check (Measuring the loop gain by injecting into the duty). The 2p2z's crossover, at 8 kHz, lies in the region where the model is reliable. In the boost and the buck-boost, with the right-half-plane zero, the same method gave 0.2 dB and 1.4° and 0.4 dB and 1.9°, from 300 Hz to 7 kHz.
