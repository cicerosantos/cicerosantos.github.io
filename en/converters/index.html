    Concepts

    • Duty ratio and ripple of the buck in CCMIn steady state, the volt-second balance on the inductor gives the buck's duty ratio, and the triangular current gives the current and voltage ripple.
    • Four-switch buck-boostTwo legs and one inductor that step the voltage down or up; the buck, boost and buck-boost modes, the plant that changes with the input and the mode the lab uses.
    • The buck's transfer function Gvd(s)G_{vd}(s)Gvd​(s)The buck's plant in CCM, from duty ratio to output voltage, is a second-order system with the LC resonance, a load-dependent Q and the ESR zero.
    • The ESR zeroThe output capacitor's series resistance adds a zero to the plant; with electrolytics it helps the loop, with ceramics it lies above the switching frequency and does not count.
    • The right-half-plane zeroIn the boost and the buck-boost, raising the duty first pulls the output down; this right-half-plane zero, at R·D'²/L, limits the crossover frequency.
    • Transfer function Gvd(s)G_{vd}(s)Gvd​(s) of the boostThe boost's plant in CCM has a DC gain of about V/D', a pair of poles that comes down with D' and a right-half-plane zero; the ADC also sees an ESR term and the firmware's delay.

    Derivations

    • The averaged switch modelReplacing the switch pair by its average over one period turns the buck into a continuous circuit; perturbed and linearized, it gives the small-signal model.
