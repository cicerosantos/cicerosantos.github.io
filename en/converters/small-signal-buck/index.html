The buck's transfer function Gvd(s)G_{vd}(s)Gvd​(s)

The buck's plant in CCM, from duty ratio to output voltage, is a second-order system with the LC resonance, a load-dependent Q and the ESR zero.

Quality factor

QQQ measures how lightly damped the pole pair is. On the Bode plot, the magnitude rises about 20log⁡10Q20\log_{10} Q20log10​Q dB above the DC gain at f0f_0f0​, and the phase drops 180° over a band about f0/Qf_0/Qf0​/Q wide. In time, the response rings at f0f_0f0​ and decays with a time constant 2Q/ω02Q/\omega_02Q/ω0​.

Two sources of damping add like conductances: the load in parallel with the capacitor, with QR=R/Z0Q_R = R/Z_0QR​=R/Z0​, and the series resistances, with Qs=Z0/(Rs+RESR)Q_s = Z_0/(R_s + R_{\mathrm{ESR}})Qs​=Z0​/(Rs​+RESR​). Hence the dependence on the load:

  • heavy load (small R) damps more, and QQQ falls;
  • light load (large R) damps less, and QQQ rises up to the limit Z0/(Rs+RESR)Z_0/(R_s + R_{\mathrm{ESR}})Z0​/(Rs​+RESR​), set by the losses alone;
  • the DC gain and f0f_0f0​ barely change with the load.

Above f0f_0f0​, the magnitude falls 40 dB per decade and the phase stays near −180°. This is the phase the compensator must lift, with zeros near the resonance, for there to be phase margin at crossover (see Loop gain, crossover frequency and margins and The 2p2z compensator).

The peak depends on the load

Design the compensator at the lightest load the converter will see. That is where the resonant peak is highest, and where the loop gain may cross 0 dB again with little phase.

The ESR zero

The factor 1+s/ωESR1 + s/\omega_{\mathrm{ESR}}1+s/ωESR​ in the numerator is the ESR zero: above fESRf_{\mathrm{ESR}}fESR​, the capacitor becomes a resistor, and the magnitude stops falling at 40 dB per decade. With ceramic capacitors, this zero lies above the switching frequency and does not help the control.

The plant the digital loop sees

Gvd(s)G_{vd}(s)Gvd​(s) is an analog plant. In the microcontroller, the ADC samples the output and the new duty only applies in the next period. The plant seen by the controller is Gvd(s) e−s(1+D)TsG_{vd}(s)\,e^{-s(1+D)T_s}Gvd​(s)e−s(1+D)Ts​; the delay does not change the magnitude, but it removes phase, more so at higher frequencies (see Computation and PWM delay).
