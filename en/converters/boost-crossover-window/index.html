The boost's crossover window

Between a floor of three times the resonance and a ceiling at a fraction of the right-half-plane zero; without losses, the ratio of the two is the plant's Q, and the 1/3 window exists only with Q above 9.

It is the lossless Q

The losses damp the peak, but hardly move the ratio. At 12 V and 2 A, the exact QQQ is 3.6, and fRHP/f0f_{\mathrm{RHP}}/f_0fRHP​/f0​ = 10.0, close to the lossless QQQ, 10.3. The QQQ read from the peak of the Bode plot gives the wrong answer.

Where the 2p2z crosses

The 3f03f_03f0​ floor assumes the zeros at f0f_0f0​. The boost's 2p2z puts both zeros at 500 Hz, below the lowest resonance of the range (570 Hz, at 10 V). At 1.5 kHz, three times above them, they have already given the same 143°. That is why it crosses at about 2.2 f02.2\,f_02.2f0​, below the floor, with 35° to 46° of margin. Zeros below f0f_0f0​ lower the floor: what counts is the distance between the crossover and the zeros.

At the two 2 A corners, the crossover lies between the two ceiling rules: fRHP/4.6f_{\mathrm{RHP}}/4.6fRHP​/4.6 at 12 V and fRHP/3.7f_{\mathrm{RHP}}/3.7fRHP​/3.7 at 10 V.

From 1/3 to 1/5 by the gain alone

With the window empty, lowering the crossover looks like the way out. At the worst corner, 10 V and 2 A, the zero is at 4.66 kHz. With the 2p2z's zeros and pole fixed, a new gain takes the crossover from fRHP/3f_{\mathrm{RHP}}/3fRHP​/3 = 1553 Hz to fRHP/5f_{\mathrm{RHP}}/5fRHP​/5 = 932 Hz:

The zero, the delay and the resonance give back 19.6°, and the compensator loses 19.2°, because the zeros at 500 Hz are close to the new crossover. Only the gain margin improves, from 10.0 to 18.0 dB. With the zeros moved down to 300 Hz together with the crossover, the model gives 56.4° and 17.1 dB.
