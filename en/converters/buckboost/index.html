Four-switch buck-boost 8–16 V → 12 V: worked example

The lab's buck-boost, from the specifications to the firmware, with a 2p2z that works from 9 to 15 V and from 1 to 3 A, and the thirteen scenarios simulated in QSPICE.

This converter steps the voltage of an 8 to 16 V battery down or up to a 12 V bus. It runs in buck-boost mode, with all four switches commutating, and is controlled by the same C firmware as the Synchronous buck 24 V → 9 V: worked example and the Synchronous boost 12 V → 48 V: worked example. The new difficulty is that the plant changes with the input, and a single compensator has to work across the whole range. Each step below points to the note that explains the theory and shows the number it gives here.

Design summary. Voltage-mode control, one sample and one update per period. The default compensator is a 2p2z with crossover at 3 kHz at the nominal point (12 V, 2 A), two zeros at 1 kHz (below the lowest resonance, 1.45 kHz at 9 V) and a pole at 40 kHz. Across the scenarios' range, the crossover stays between 2.4 and 3.6 kHz, and the phase margin between 38° and 43°.

In the converters

The buck-boost's right-half-plane zero lies higher than the lab's Synchronous boost 12 V → 48 V: worked example's (20.9 against 6.9 kHz, at 2 A): the product D′VdD'V_dD′Vd​ is nearly the same in both, but here the inductor carries half the current (4.05 against 8.14 A) and is smaller (22 against 33 µH). That is why the 2p2z crosses twice as high, at 3 kHz. The price of buck-boost mode is a different one: the plant changes with the input, and the compensator is chosen for the 9 V, 3 A corner, not for the nominal point.

Exercise

With 12 V at the output and a 2 A load, what is the approximate RMS current through Q1 in buck-boost mode, with 12 V at the input?

Answer

Q1 carries the inductor current during DDD: IQ1≈ILD=4.050.506=2.9I_{Q1} \approx I_L\sqrt{D} = 4.05\sqrt{0.506} = 2.9IQ1​≈IL​D​=4.050.506​=2.9 A RMS, neglecting the ripple. In buck mode, with Q1 carrying 2 A for nearly the whole period, it would be about 2 A: buck-boost mode doubles the inductor current and increases the conduction losses.
