Computation and PWM delay

From the ADC sample to the PWM edge it moves, (1+D) Ts(1 + D)\,T_s(1+D)Ts​ go by in this firmware, and this delay costs 360∘ f (1+D) Ts360^\circ\, f\,(1 + D)\,T_s360∘f(1+D)Ts​ of phase at the crossover.

First, see: Loop gain, crossover frequency and margins, Sampling and the zero-order hold (ZOH)

In the analog loop, a change at the output reaches the PWM comparator almost at once. In the digital one, the information goes through a chain: the ADC converts, the interrupt computes, the PWM register is updated and only then does one edge of the pulse move. Each stage delays the response, and a pure delay tdt_dtd​ is e−stde^{-s t_d}e−std​: it does not change the magnitude of the loop gain, but it costs phase.

The chain in this site's firmware

Period kkk starts at t=kTst = kT_st=kTs​:

  1. Sample and conversion. The timer triggers the ADC at kTskT_skTs​, at the period start (Sampling and the zero-order hold (ZOH)): that is what the buck's frames do and, with SAMPLE_PHASE = 0, the boost and buck-boost scenarios. The trigger latency and the sampling window add up to 200 to 220 ns, which do not enter (1+D)Ts(1+D)T_s(1+D)Ts​: the trigger and the choice of instant are in ADC trigger and sample instant, an optional detour that builds on the boost of Sampling instant and the ESR in the boost.
  2. Computation. The end-of-conversion interrupt runs the PID or the 2p2z and writes the new duty cycle to the compare register (CCR).
  3. Update in the next period. With preload on, the new CCR only takes effect at the next update event, at (k+1)Ts(k+1)T_s(k+1)Ts​. That guarantees a whole pulse with the old value, even if the computation is slow, provided it finishes before the update event, and it costs one period.
  4. The modulated edge. In trailing-edge PWM (sawtooth carrier), the switch turns on at the start of the period and off at (k+1+D)Ts(k+1+D)T_s(k+1+D)Ts​. Only that edge depends on ddd; the duty-cycle perturbation enters the plant there, DTsDT_sDTs​ after the start.

Other timings

  • Sample away from the period start. With preload kept, moving the sample shortens the delay to (1+D−φ) Ts(1 + D - \varphi)\,T_s(1+D−φ)Ts​ (ADC trigger and sample instant).
  • Center-aligned PWM. The centered pulse on the H7 (sample at the reset, load at the next reset) is in Centered PWM on the H7: case B, and its delay is not (1+D) Ts(1+D)\,T_s(1+D)Ts​.
  • Computation that misses the deadline. If the interrupt ends after the update event, the value only takes effect one period later: (2+D)Ts(2+D)T_s(2+D)Ts​.

Jitter and priorities

The delay is only (1+D)Ts(1+D)T_s(1+D)Ts​ if the control interrupt never misses its deadline. A higher-priority communication interrupt, slow flash access or a debugging printf can push the CCR write past the update event in some periods, and the loop then has a varying delay. Give the control the highest priority. The deadline and how to measure it are in Interrupt deadline and budget.

Phase budget

Before choosing fcf_cfc​, work out the phase the delay will cost. If it exceeds about 30°, the crossover is too high for the chosen timing.

The model with (1+D)Ts(1+D)T_s(1+D)Ts​ was checked in QSPICE, adding a small sine wave to the duty cycle inside the firmware: from 500 Hz to 15 kHz, it stayed within 0.3 dB and 0.7° of the simulation (Measuring the loop gain by injecting into the duty).

For the boost and the buck-boost, the firmware's delay is also (1+D) Ts(1 + D)\,T_s(1+D)Ts​, but the switched circuit's response behaves, to first order, like a delay of (1+D/2) Ts(1 + D/2)\,T_s(1+D/2)Ts​ (Sampling instant and the ESR in the boost). Delaying G1's edge deprives the capacitor of the inductor current at its peak, not of its average: it is as if the perturbation entered at the middle of G1's pulse. In the buck, the jump at the edge does not depend on the state, and (1+D) Ts(1 + D)\,T_s(1+D)Ts​ is exact. The site does not use a fitted delay for these two: it uses the sampled-data model of the switched circuit, which stays within 0.2 dB and 1.4° of QSPICE from 300 Hz to 7 kHz. With the crossover at 1.5 kHz in the boost, the delay matters little; there the limit is the The right-half-plane zero.

Exercise 1

At what frequency does the buck's (1+D)Ts(1+D)T_s(1+D)Ts​ delay alone consume 45° of phase?

Answer

f=45/(360×6.875 µs)=18.2f = 45/(360 \times 6.875\ \text{µs}) = 18.2f=45/(360×6.875 µs)=18.2 kHz: exactly the region of the aggressive design, which crosses at 18 kHz with a 16° margin.
