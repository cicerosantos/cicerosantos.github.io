The 2p2z compensator

The two-pole, two-zero compensator, a digital relative of the type III, designed by its poles and zeros, and its relation with the PID; with the buck's default design.

First, see: Loop gain, crossover frequency and margins, The bilinear (Tustin) transform and prewarping

The buck's plant, above the LC resonance, has a phase near −180∘-180^\circ−180∘ (The buck's transfer function Gvd(s)G_{vd}(s)Gvd​(s)), and the digital delay takes even more (Computation and PWM delay). An integrator alone would add −90∘-90^\circ−90∘, and the loop would have no margin. The compensator needs an integrator, to drive the steady-state error to zero, and two zeros, to give phase back at the crossover. That is the 2p2z: two poles, two zeros.

The two poles are the integrator, at z=1z = 1z=1, and a real high-frequency pole ppp. It is the digital version of the analog type III compensator, which has two high-frequency poles instead of one.

Design by poles and zeros

Each root has a job, and the design is done on the Bode plot of the loop gain:

  • Integrator (z=1z = 1z=1): infinite gain at DC, zero steady-state error at the sample.
  • Two zeros near or below the LC resonance: each gives back up to +90∘+90^\circ+90∘. Below the resonance, they already give almost all their lead when the plant's phase plunges. Lower zeros give more phase at fcf_cfc​, but they lower the gain between the integrator and the resonance, and the loop becomes slow to correct errors in that band.
  • High-frequency pole: limits the gain above the crossover, where there is only ripple and quantization noise (ADC quantization). When the ESR zero falls inside the band, the pole is placed on it to cancel it (The ESR zero).
  • Gain KKK: chosen last, so that ∣TL∣=1|T_L| = 1∣TL​∣=1 at fcf_cfc​.

The relation with the PID

At 8 kHz, the compensator gives +52∘+52^\circ+52∘ of phase, which take the plant with the delay, at −191∘-191^\circ−191∘, to −139∘-139^\circ−139∘: hence the 41°. In QSPICE, the load step from 1 to 2 A pulls the output down to 8.59 V; it overshoots to 9.14 V on the way back and stays within 50 mV of its final value from about 130 µs after the step.

In the boost, the 2p2z follows the same recipe, with the other converter's numbers: zeros at 500 Hz, below the 690 Hz resonance, a pole at 30 kHz and a crossover at 1.5 kHz, low because of the The right-half-plane zero.
