The 2p2z compensator

The two-pole, two-zero compensator, a digital relative of the type III, designed by its poles and zeros, and its relation with the PID; with the buck's default design.

First, see: Loop gain, crossover frequency and margins, The bilinear (Tustin) transform and prewarping, PI, phase lead and the type III

The buck's plant, above the LC resonance, has a phase near −180∘-180^\circ−180∘ (The buck's transfer function Gvd(s)G_{vd}(s)Gvd​(s)), and the digital delay takes even more (Computation and PWM delay). An integrator alone would add −90∘-90^\circ−90∘ and leave no margin; the compensator needs one, to drive the steady-state error to zero, and two zeros, to give phase back at the crossover. That is the 2p2z: two poles, two zeros.

The poles are the integrator, at z=1z = 1z=1, and a real high-frequency pole ppp: it is the digital version of the analog type III, which has two high-frequency poles.

Design by poles and zeros

Each root has a job on the Bode plot of the loop gain:

  • Integrator (z=1z = 1z=1): infinite gain at DC, zero steady-state error at the sample.
  • Two zeros near or below the LC resonance: each gives back up to +90∘+90^\circ+90∘. Below it, they already give almost all their lead when the plant's phase plunges. Lower zeros give more phase at fcf_cfc​, but they lower the gain between the integrator and the resonance, and the loop corrects errors in that band slowly. On the pair, the zeros would cancel it, with the risks of Pole-zero cancellation and its risks.
  • High-frequency pole: limits the gain above the crossover, where there is only ripple and quantization noise (ADC quantization). If the ESR zero falls inside the band, the pole cancels it (The ESR zero).
  • Gain KKK: chosen last, so that ∣T∣=1|T| = 1∣T∣=1 at fcf_cfc​.
