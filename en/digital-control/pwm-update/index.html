PWM update: trailing edge vs symmetric

How a digital PWM takes the new duty from the CCR's preload to the edge it moves, and the delay that follows, td=tc+tmt_d = t_c + t_mtd​=tc​+tm​: "half-period ZOH plus z−1z^{-1}z−1" and "(1+D) Ts(1 + D)\,T_s(1+D)Ts​" are the same model for different PWMs; adding the two counts the modulator twice.

First, see: Sampling and the zero-order hold (ZOH), Computation and PWM delay

In a digital PWM, the firmware does not move the edge: it writes the new duty to the CCR's preload (the shadow register), and a timer update event copies it to the active register (PWM timer and shadow registers). In a trailing-edge PWM, with a sawtooth, the update is the counter reset; in a symmetric PWM, with a triangle, it is the peak, the valley or both. Only then does the edge move, and the loop's delay is the time from the sample to that edge.

The reference course counts this delay as "ZOH, −ωTs/2-\omega T_s/2−ωTs​/2, plus z−1z^{-1}z−1 when the update goes to the next cycle". This site counts (1+D) Ts(1 + D)\,T_s(1+D)Ts​ (Computation and PWM delay). Both counts are right, each for its own PWM; the error is to mix them.

One delay, two parts

With one update per period, the delay to the plant is

td=tc+tm,t_d = t_c + t_m,td​=tc​+tm​,

where tct_ctc​ runs from the sample to the update event, when the new duty takes effect, and tmt_mtm​ from there to the edge it moves (or to the midpoint of the two, when both move). In small signals, the edge shifted by d^ Ts\hat d\,T_sd^Ts​ adds to the switched voltage a pulse of height VinV_{in}Vin​ and width d^ Ts\hat d\,T_sd^Ts​: to first order, an impulse at the edge's instant. So the modulator acts as a pure delay tmt_mtm​, with exact phase in the baseband; the folded terms are negligible in the buck up to about fs/10f_s/10fs​/10.

In an analog PWM, the edge follows the instantaneous modulating signal, and tmt_mtm​ does not exist: the modulator is only the gain 1/VM1/V_M1/VM​.

Symmetric PWM, update at the peak or valley

With a triangle carrier and the load at a peak or a valley, the two edges it moves lie symmetric about the carrier's next extreme, Ts/2T_s/2Ts​/2 after the load, for any DDD. So tm=Ts/2t_m = T_s/2tm​=Ts​/2: the other course's "ZOH" is the modulator, not one more block. The on pulse is centered on the valley, so sampling there is away from the switching edges. Three timings:

Computation and update tct_ctc​ tdt_dtd​
sample at the valley, no preload (immediate write) the computation Ts/2T_s/2Ts​/2
sample at the valley, load at the peak (preload) Ts/2T_s/2Ts​/2 TsT_sTs​
load at the next peak or valley TsT_sTs​ 1.5 Ts1.5\,T_s1.5Ts​

In the first row, the load is the write itself: tm=Ts/2−tct_m = T_s/2 - t_ctm​=Ts​/2−tc​, and the sum stays Ts/2T_s/2Ts​/2 if the write lands before the falling edge, DTs/2DT_s/2DTs​/2 after the valley. At small DDD there is hardly any time, and a late write delays or loses the edge; that is why the preload is the usual setup. In the middle row, the sample at the valley and the load at the next peak give tc=Ts/2t_c = T_s/2tc​=Ts​/2; the edges' midpoint comes Ts/2T_s/2Ts​/2 later, and td=Tst_d = T_std​=Ts​.

With double update, at every peak and valley, Ts=TPWM/2T_s = T_{PWM}/2Ts​=TPWM​/2. Counting twice

Adding the ZOH (Ts/2T_s/2Ts​/2) to (1+D) Ts(1 + D)\,T_s(1+D)Ts​ gives (1.5+D) Ts(1.5 + D)\,T_s(1.5+D)Ts​: the modulator goes in twice, as the ZOH and as DTsDT_sDTs​. In the symmetric PWM, the same error is adding a "PWM delay" of Ts/2T_s/2Ts​/2 to the ZOH. Count tct_ctc​ once and tmt_mtm​ once.
