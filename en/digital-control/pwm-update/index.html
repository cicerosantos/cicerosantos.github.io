PWM update: trailing edge vs symmetric

How a digital PWM takes the new duty from the CCR's preload to the edge it moves, and the delay that follows, td=tc+tmt_d = t_c + t_mtd​=tc​+tm​: "half-period ZOH plus z−1z^{-1}z−1" and "(1+D) Ts(1 + D)\,T_s(1+D)Ts​" are the same model for different PWMs; adding the two counts the modulator twice.

First, see: Sampling and the zero-order hold (ZOH), Computation and PWM delay

In a digital PWM, the firmware does not move the edge: it writes the new duty to the CCR's preload (the shadow register), and a timer update event copies that value to the active register, the one the carrier is compared with. In a trailing-edge PWM, with a sawtooth carrier, the update is the counter reset. In a symmetric PWM, with a triangle carrier, it is the peak, the valley or both. Only then does the edge move, and the loop's delay is the time from the sample to that edge. The figure shows both cases, period by period.

The reference course counts this delay as "ZOH, −ωTs/2-\omega T_s/2−ωTs​/2, plus z−1z^{-1}z−1 when the update goes to the next cycle", without adding a "PWM delay" to the ZOH. This site counts (1+D) Ts(1 + D)\,T_s(1+D)Ts​ (Computation and PWM delay). Both counts are right, each for its own PWM; the error is to mix them.

One delay, two parts

In a digital PWM with one update per period, the delay from the sample to the plant is

td=tc+tm,t_d = t_c + t_m,td​=tc​+tm​,

where tct_ctc​ runs from the sample to the instant the new duty takes effect (the update event), and tmt_mtm​ from that instant to the edge it moves (or to the midpoint of the two edges, when both move). In small signals, the edge shifted by d^ Ts\hat d\,T_sd^Ts​ adds to the switched voltage a pulse of height VinV_{in}Vin​ and width d^ Ts\hat d\,T_sd^Ts​; to first order, an impulse at the edge's instant. So the modulator acts as a pure delay tmt_mtm​, with exact phase in the baseband term. What is left are folded terms, negligible in the buck up to about fs/10f_s/10fs​/10.

In an analog PWM, the edge responds to the instantaneous value of the modulating signal, and this tmt_mtm​ does not exist: the modulator has only its gain 1/VM1/V_M1/VM​.

Symmetric PWM, update at the peak or valley

With a triangle carrier and the load done at a peak or a valley, the two edges it moves lie symmetric about the carrier's next extreme, Ts/2T_s/2Ts​/2 after the load, for any DDD. So tm=Ts/2t_m = T_s/2tm​=Ts​/2: the other course's "ZOH" is the modulator, not one more block. In the figure, the switch is on while the carrier is below the CCR, so the on pulse is centred on the valley: sampling at the valley samples mid on-pulse, away from the switching edges. Counting twice

Adding the ZOH, half a period (Ts/2T_s/2Ts​/2), to (1+D) Ts(1 + D)\,T_s(1+D)Ts​ gives (1.5+D) Ts(1.5 + D)\,T_s(1.5+D)Ts​: the modulator goes in twice, once as the ZOH and once as DTsDT_sDTs​. In the symmetric PWM, the same error is adding a "PWM delay" of Ts/2T_s/2Ts​/2 to the ZOH. In any PWM, count tct_ctc​ once and tmt_mtm​ once.
