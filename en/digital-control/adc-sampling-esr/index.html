Sampling instant and the ESR in the boost

In the boost and the buck-boost, the sample taken just before G1 turns on sits above the output's average and reads an ESR term that changes the plant; with it, the effective delay that fits QSPICE is (1 + 0.6·D)·Tₛ.

First, see: Sampling and the zero-order hold (ZOH), Transfer function Gvd(s)G_{vd}(s)Gvd​(s) of the boost

The averaged model describes the output's average over a period. The ADC does not read the average: it reads the voltage at one instant, and what that instant sees depends on the waveform. In the buck, the sample at the start of the period falls near the ripple's valley, a few millivolts below the average (Sampling and the zero-order hold (ZOH)). In the boost and the buck-boost, the difference is larger and, besides, it changes with the duty and with the current: it becomes part of the plant.

What the sample reads

This site's firmware triggers the ADC at the start of each period, just before G1 turns on. In the boost, G1 is the main switch: until then, G2 was conducting, and the capacitor received the inductor current minus the load's. Two effects add up:

  1. The capacitor ripple. While G1 conducts, only the capacitor feeds the load, and the voltage falls by ΔvC=IoDTs/C\Delta v_C = I_o D T_s/CΔvC​=Io​DTs​/C; while G2 conducts, it rises again. The sample catches the end of the rise, the peak: ΔvC/2\Delta v_C/2ΔvC​/2 above the average.
  2. The ESR drop. On average, the capacitor receives d′ iL−io=0d'\,i_L - i_o = 0d′iL​−io​=0. At the sampling instant, it receives iL−ioi_L - i_oiL​−io​: the whole inductor current, d iLd\,i_LdiL​ more than the average. The sample reads RESR d iLR_{ESR}\,d\,i_LRESR​diL​ more, minus the valley of the current ripple.

Plant seen by the ADC

The transfer function from the duty to the sample, not to the average output. It is the one that enters the loop gain. In this site's boost and buck-boost, it is GvdG_{vd}Gvd​ plus the ESR term, times the firmware's delay.

The RESR I d^R_{ESR}\,I\,\hat dRESR​Id^ term does not fall with frequency. Well above the resonance, the −I(sL+Rl)-I(sL + R_l)−I(sL+Rl​) term leaves the average output at about −I Zo d^-I\,Z_o\,\hat d−IZo​d^, which includes −I RESR d^-I\,R_{ESR}\,\hat d−IRESR​d^; the sample cancels that part, and the right-half-plane zero moves up. The plant seen by the ADC gains a little phase near the crossover, and a design made with the average stays on the conservative side.

The effective delay

The delay from the sample to the edge it moves is (1+D) Ts(1 + D)\,T_s(1+D)Ts​ in the buck (Computation and PWM delay). In the boost and the buck-boost, the delay that best fits the QSPICE measurements, with the ESR term already in the model, is shorter:

td=(1+0.6 D) Tst_d = (1 + 0.6\,D)\,T_std​=(1+0.6D)Ts​

The 0.6 factor was found by fitting, comparing the model with the loop gain measured on the switched circuit (Measuring the loop gain by injecting into the duty), not derived. The firmware's delay is still (1+D) Ts(1 + D)\,T_s(1+D)Ts​; the smaller factor absorbs what the averaged model, even with the ESR term, does not yet describe, and should not be read as a physical delay. It holds for this firmware and this sampling instant.

Why the instant matters

The sampling instant decides three things: the offset between the regulated value and the average, the term added to the plant and the delay to the edge. Sampling in the middle of the interval in which G1 conducts, for example, catches the capacitor halfway down its fall, near the ripple's average, and the ESR with the constant current −io-i_o−io​, without the i^L\hat i_Li^L​ term. In exchange, the ADC trigger has to follow the duty, with a second compare channel of the timer, and less time is left for the computation.

In the converters

Boost. At 1 A, the inductor current has a valley of 2.68 A, and at the sampling instant the ESR adds 10 mΩ×(2.68−1)=1710\,\text{m}\Omega \times (2.68 - 1) = 1710mΩ×(2.68−1)=17 mV. The capacitor ripple is 1 A×0.752×10 µs/100 µF=751\,\text{A} \times 0.752 \times 10\,\text{µs}/100\,\text{µF} = 751A×0.752×10µs/100µF=75 mV, and the peak sits 38 mV above the average. The sample should sit about 54 mV above the average. In QSPICE, with the samples regulated at 48.00 V, the output's average before the step is 47.95 V:

In the plant, the right-half-plane zero goes from 14.1 to 22.1 kHz at 1 A and from 6.9 to 8.4 kHz at 2 A. At the 2p2z's crossover, the ESR term adds 1.6° of margin: 45.3° instead of 43.7° at 1 A, and 40.6° instead of 39.0° at 2 A. The delay is (1+0.6×0.752)×10 µs=14.5(1 + 0.6 \times 0.752) \times 10\,\text{µs} = 14.5(1+0.6×0.752)×10µs=14.5 µs, and it costs 7.8° at 1.5 kHz. With (1+D) Ts(1 + D)\,T_s(1+D)Ts​, it would cost 1.6° more. With both terms, the model stayed within 0.2 dB and 1.4° of QSPICE from 300 Hz to 7 kHz, at 1 and 2 A.

Buck-boost. At 12 V and 2 A, the capacitor ripple is 2×0.506×10 µs/100 µF=1012 \times 0.506 \times 10\,\text{µs}/100\,\text{µF} = 1012×0.506×10µs/100µF=101 mV, and the peak sits 51 mV above the average; the ESR adds another 7 mV.
