Sampling instant and the ESR in the boost

In the boost and the buck-boost, the sample taken just before G1 turns on sits above the output's average and reads an ESR term that changes the plant; and the switched response behaves, to first order, like a delay of (1 + D/2)·Tₛ, not (1 + D)·Tₛ.

First, see: Sampling and the zero-order hold (ZOH), Transfer function Gvd(s)G_{vd}(s)Gvd​(s) of the boost

The ADC does not read the output's average over a period, which the averaged model describes: it reads the voltage at one instant, and its value there depends on the waveform. In the boost and the buck-boost, the difference is larger and changes with the duty and with the current: it becomes part of the plant.

What the sample reads

This site's firmware triggers the ADC at the start of each period, just before G1 turns on. In the boost, G1 is the main switch: until then, G2 was conducting, and the capacitor received the inductor current minus the load's. Two effects add up:

  1. The capacitor ripple. While G1 conducts, only the capacitor feeds the load, and the voltage falls by ΔvC=IoDTs/C\Delta v_C = I_o D T_s/CΔvC​=Io​DTs​/C; while G2 conducts, it rises again. The sample catches the end of the rise, the peak: ΔvC/2\Delta v_C/2ΔvC​/2 above the average.
  2. The ESR drop. On average, the capacitor receives d′ iL−io=0d'\,i_L - i_o = 0d′iL​−io​=0. At the sampling instant, it receives iL−ioi_L - i_oiL​−io​: the whole inductor current, d iLd\,i_LdiL​ more than the average. The sample reads RESR d iLR_{ESR}\,d\,i_LRESR​diL​ more, minus the valley of the current ripple.

The first effect is nearly constant and only shifts the regulated value: the loop regulates the sample, and the average sits below it. The second changes with ddd and with iLi_LiL​, and its linearization enters the plant:

Plant seen by the ADC

The transfer function from the duty to the sample, not to the average output. It is the one that enters the loop gain. In this site's boost and buck-boost, it is, to first order, GvdG_{vd}Gvd​ plus the ESR term, times a delay of (1+D/2) Ts(1 + D/2)\,T_s(1+D/2)Ts​.

The RESR I d^R_{ESR}\,I\,\hat dRESR​Id^ term does not fall with frequency. Well above the resonance, the −I(sL+Rl)-I(sL + R_l)−I(sL+Rl​) term leaves the average output at about −I Zo d^-I\,Z_o\,\hat d−IZo​d^, which includes −I RESR d^-I\,R_{ESR}\,\hat d−IRESR​d^; the sample cancels that part, and the right-half-plane zero moves up. The plant seen by the ADC gains a little phase near the crossover, and a design made with the average stays on the conservative side.

The effective delay

The delay from the sample to the edge it moves is (1+D) Ts(1 + D)\,T_s(1+D)Ts​ in the buck (Computation and PWM delay). In the boost and the buck-boost, the firmware has the same delay, but the switched circuit's response behaves, to first order, like a shorter delay:

td≈(1+D/2) Tst_d \approx (1 + D/2)\,T_std​≈(1+D/2)Ts​

Delaying G1's edge deprives the capacitor of the inductor current at its peak, not of its average. At the edge, the state is half a ripple away from the average (iLi_LiL​ at its peak, vCv_CvC​ at its valley), and the effect is that of a perturbation at the middle of G1's pulse, not at the edge. In the buck, the circuits of G1 and G2 have the same dynamics, the jump at the edge does not depend on the state, and (1+D) Ts(1 + D)\,T_s(1+D)Ts​ is exact.

The site does not use this approximate delay: in the boost's and buck-boost's Bode plots, the plant is the sampled-data model of the switched circuit, with no fitted factor. In each period, the state (iLi_LiL​, vCv_CvC​) goes through the circuits of G1 and G2; the duty change enters as a jump at the falling edge, computed with the steady state there; the sample reads vCv_CvC​ plus the drop on the ESR, and the period of computation adds z−1z^{-1}z−1.

Against QSPICE (Measuring the loop gain by injecting into the duty), from 300 Hz to 7 kHz, this model stays within 0.2 dB and 1.4° in the boost (12 V at 1, 2 and 4 A) and within 0.2 dB and 0.7° in the buck-boost (12 V and 1 A, 12 V and 2 A, 9 V and 3 A). Almost all of the rest comes from the dead time, which the model leaves out. The (1+D/2) Ts(1 + D/2)\,T_s(1+D/2)Ts​ is not a physical delay (the firmware's is still (1+D) Ts(1 + D)\,T_s(1+D)Ts​) and holds only for this firmware and this sample, at the period start. Another instant changes the offset, the ESR term and the delay (ADC trigger and sample instant).

In the converters

Boost. At 1 A, the inductor current has a valley of 2.68 A, and at the sampling instant the ESR adds 10 mΩ×(2.68−1)=1710\,\text{m}\Omega \times (2.68 - 1) = 1710mΩ×(2.68−1)=17 mV. The capacitor ripple is 1 A×0.752×10 µs/100 µF=751\,\text{A} \times 0.752 \times 10\,\text{µs}/100\,\text{µF} = 751A×0.752×10µs/100µF=75 mV, and the peak sits 38 mV above the average. The sample should sit about 54 mV above the average. In QSPICE, with the samples regulated at 48.00 V, the output's average before the step is 47.95 V:

In the plant, the right-half-plane zero goes from 14.1 to 22.1 kHz at 1 A and from 6.9 to 8.4 kHz at 2 A. At the 2p2z's crossover, the ESR term adds 1.6° of margin, at 1 and at 2 A. The equivalent delay is (1+0.752/2)×10 µs=13.8(1 + 0.752/2) \times 10\,\text{µs} = 13.8(1+0.752/2)×10µs=13.8 µs, and it costs 7.4° at 1.5 kHz. With (1+D) Ts(1 + D)\,T_s(1+D)Ts​, it would cost 2.0° more. The sampled-data model, with both effects, gives 46.2° at 1 A and 41.5° at 2 A. In it, removing the ESR from the sample costs about 2°: the count is another one, with the inductor current at the sampling instant, not the term linearized around the average that gives the 1.6° in the averaged model.

Buck-boost. At 12 V and 2 A, the capacitor ripple is 2×0.506×10 µs/100 µF=101.22 \times 0.506 \times 10\,\text{µs}/100\,\text{µF} = 101.22×0.506×10µs/100µF=101.2 mV, and the peak sits 50.6 mV above the average. The inductor current's valley is 2.67 A in the model, and the ESR adds another 10 mΩ×(2.67−2)=6.710\,\text{m}\Omega \times (2.67 - 2) = 6.710mΩ×(2.67−2)=6.7 mV. In the scenarios, the average before the reference step is 11.95 V. The ESR term takes the zero from 20.9 to 28.9 kHz and adds 1.2° of margin to the 2p2z.
