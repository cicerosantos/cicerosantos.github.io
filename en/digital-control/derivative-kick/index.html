Derivative kick and derivative on the measurement

On a reference step, a derivative term computed on the error jumps in a single period and saturates the duty; differentiating the measurement takes the reference out of that term.

First, see: Anti-windup and duty saturation, The 2p2z compensator

The saturation of Anti-windup and duty saturation needs a trigger. On a reference step, the fastest one is the PID's derivative term. This note shows the size of that jump, the derivative kick, and how to remove it without changing the loop's margins.

1. The kick

The buck's PID (buck_pid.cpp) and that of scenario.cpp, in the boost and the buck-boost, compute the derivative on the error e=vref−ve = v_{ref} - ve=vref​−v, with a filter of time constant TFT_FTF​:

dD[k]=TF dD[k−1]+KD (e[k]−e[k−1])TF+Ts.d_D[k] = \frac{T_F\,d_D[k-1] + K_D\,\bigl(e[k] - e[k-1]\bigr)}{T_F + T_s}.dD​[k]=TF​+Ts​TF​dD​[k−1]+KD​(e[k]−e[k−1])​.

Without a filter, with TF=0T_F = 0TF​=0, as in the buck's PID, this is KD (e[k]−e[k−1])/TsK_D\,(e[k] - e[k-1])/T_sKD​(e[k]−e[k−1])/Ts​. On a reference step Δ\DeltaΔ, the output has not changed yet, and the error jumps by Δ\DeltaΔ from one sample to the next. In the first period, the derivative is

dD=KDTF+Ts Δ.d_D = \frac{K_D}{T_F + T_s}\,\Delta .dD​=TF​+Ts​KD​​Δ.

Without a filter, the jump lasts one period: in the next one, the error barely changes, and the term returns to zero. With a filter, it decays by the factor p=TF/(TF+Ts)p = T_F/(T_F + T_s)p=TF​/(TF​+Ts​) each period, the PID's pole. Added to the proportional term, KP ΔK_P\,\DeltaKP​Δ, it usually exceeds the duty's limits.

2. Derivative on the measurement

With a constant reference, e[k]−e[k−1]=−(v[k]−v[k−1])e[k] - e[k-1] = -(v[k] - v[k-1])e[k]−e[k−1]=−(v[k]−v[k−1]). Differentiating the measurement instead of the error gives the same term, and the loop gain, from the sample to the duty, does not change: the margins stay the same. What changes is the reference's path, which now enters only through the proportional and integral terms. A reference step no longer produces a derivative kick:

/* Derivative on the measurement, with the filter of scenario.cpp (TF = 0 in the buck) */
float derivative(float vmeas)
{
    deriv = (TF * deriv - KD * (vmeas - vmeas_prev)) / (TF + T);  /* no kick when vref changes */
    vmeas_prev = vmeas;
    return deriv;
}

The result takes the place of deriv in the back-calculation PID of Anti-windup and duty saturation.

The proportional term still jumps by KP ΔK_P\,\DeltaKP​Δ. And the 2p2z, which does not separate the derivative from the other terms, jumps too: its b0 e[k]b_0\,e[k]b0​e[k] term acts in full in the first period (The 2p2z compensator). For both, the remedy is the reference ramp of Soft-start and pre-biasing, which keeps the error small during the transition.

In the converters

Buck. In the pid-vref-step scenario, the reference goes from 9 to 5 V without slew. The error jumps to −4 V, and the derivative term is KD Δe/Ts=(1.5 μ/5 μ)×(−4)=−1.2K_D\,\Delta e/T_s = (1.5\ \mu/5\ \mu)\times(-4) = -1.2KD​Δe/Ts​=(1.5 μ/5 μ)×(−4)=−1.2. Added to KP e=−0.23K_P\,e = -0.23KP​e=−0.23 and to the integrator near 0.38, the command goes negative, and the duty is clamped to zero for one period. The low-side switch stays on for the whole period, and the inductor current goes negative, down to about −5 A: the high-side switch returns energy to the input. The duty then climbs back over about ten periods, and the output sinks to 4.30 V.

Exercise 1

On the buck's step from 9 to 5 V (KPK_PKP​ = 0.0565, KIK_IKI​ = 1309, KDK_DKD​ = 1.5 µs/V, no filter, TsT_sTs​ = 5 µs), what is the derivative term in the first period with the derivative on the error? And with the derivative on the measurement? Does the duty saturate in the second case?

Answer

On the error: KD Δe/Ts=0.3×(−4)=−1.2K_D\,\Delta e/T_s = 0.3 \times (-4) = -1.2KD​Δe/Ts​=0.3×(−4)=−1.2. On the measurement: the sample of the step's period still reads 9.00 V, like the previous one, and the term is practically zero. What remains is KP e=0.0565×(−4)=−0.23K_P\,e = 0.0565 \times (-4) = -0.23KP​e=0.0565×(−4)=−0.23 and the integrator, which moves by KI Ts e=1309×5 μ×(−4)=−0.026K_I\,T_s\,e = 1309 \times 5\ \mu \times (-4) = -0.026KI​Ts​e=1309×5 μ×(−4)=−0.026 and sits near 0.38−0.03=0.350.38 - 0.03 = 0.350.38−0.03=0.35. The command, −0.23+0.35≈0.13-0.23 + 0.35 \approx 0.13−0.23+0.35≈0.13, is positive: nothing saturates. With the derivative on the error, −1.2−0.23+0.35=−1.08-1.2 - 0.23 + 0.35 = -1.08−1.2−0.23+0.35=−1.08, and the duty goes to zero.

Exercise 2

In the boost (KPK_PKP​ = 0.0114, KDK_DKD​ = 1.75 µs/V, TFT_FTF​ = 7.96 µs, TsT_sTs​ = 10 µs), compute the kick per volt of jump in the error. On the step from 40 to 48 V, why does the duty saturate at 0.85, and for how many periods? Before the step, the integrator holds the steady-state command, 0.71.

Answer

KD/(TF+Ts)=1.75/(7.96+10)=0.097K_D/(T_F + T_s) = 1.75/(7.96 + 10) = 0.097KD​/(TF​+Ts​)=1.75/(7.96+10)=0.097 per volt. The error jumps by 8 V: a kick of 0.780.780.78, plus KP e=0.09K_P\,e = 0.09KP​e=0.09 and the integrator at 0.71, a command of 1.58, far above 0.85. The kick decays by the factor p=7.96/17.96=0.443p = 7.96/17.96 = 0.443p=7.96/17.96=0.443 each period: 0.35, 0.15 and 0.07, and the next commands are near 1.15, 0.96 and 0.88. That is four periods at 0.85; in the fifth, about 0.835. The frames of pid-vref-up-step show it: four periods with G1 at 0.84 (the dead time takes 0.01 off the command) and then 0.825. With the derivative on the measurement, the command would be 0.09+0.71=0.800.09 + 0.71 = 0.800.09+0.71=0.80, and nothing would saturate.
