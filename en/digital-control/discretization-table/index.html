Discretization table

The six most used maps from s to z (forward and backward Euler, Tustin, prewarped Tustin, ZOH equivalent and matched pole-zero), with stability, DC gain, high-frequency behaviour and when to use each.

First, see: The bilinear (Tustin) transform and prewarping

A controller designed in sss has to become a difference equation. The bilinear (Tustin) transform and prewarping introduced the bilinear transform, and Sampling and the zero-order hold (ZOH) introduced the zero-order hold. This table gathers those two and the other common methods, with Ts=1/fsT_s = 1/f_sTs​=1/fs​ the sampling period.

Reading the table

  • Stability: only forward Euler can make a stable pole unstable. A real pole at −ωp-\omega_p−ωp​ goes to z=1−ωpTsz = 1 - \omega_p T_sz=1−ωp​Ts​, which leaves the circle when ωpTs>2\omega_p T_s > 2ωp​Ts​>2, that is, fp>fs/πf_p > f_s/\pifp​>fs​/π.
  • DC gain: all of them keep the gain at z=1z = 1z=1, which is why the integrator stays an integrator. Matched pole-zero keeps it because the gain is chosen to.
  • Delay: the ZOH equivalent of a compensator without a direct term adds one period of delay. That is why it serves for the plant, in Sampling and the zero-order hold (ZOH), and not for the controller.
  • Frequency warping: in Tustin, it is negligible below fs/10f_s/10fs​/10 and large near fs/2f_s/2fs​/2. Prewarping fixes one frequency only (see The bilinear (Tustin) transform and prewarping).

In the converters

Buck. The default design at 200 kHz has zeros at 2 kHz and a pole at 60 kHz, and scenarios.json takes it to zzz by matched pole-zero: the pole goes to p=e−2π⋅60 k⋅Ts=0.1518p = e^{-2\pi \cdot 60\,\text{k}\cdot T_s} = 0.1518p=e−2π⋅60k⋅Ts​=0.1518, which is a2a_2a2​, and a1=−(1+p)=−1.1518a_1 = -(1 + p) = -1.1518a1​=−(1+p)=−1.1518. At the zero, at 1 % of fsf_sfs​, the four agree to two decimal places. The pole, at 30 % of fsf_sfs​, lands in four different places: forward Euler puts it near z=−1z = -1z=−1, ringing at fs/2f_s/2fs​/2, and Tustin turns it into a pole that, seen in frequency, sits at 48.1 kHz instead of 60 kHz.

The PID of scenario.cpp uses backward Euler: integ += KI * T * err adds the current error, and the derivative is KD (e[k]−e[k−1])/TsK_D\,(e[k] - e[k-1])/T_sKD​(e[k]−e[k−1])/Ts​, with its filter also discretized by backward Euler.
