From the boost to the digital loop

What changes in the digital loop when the plant has a right-half-plane zero: from the boost's averaged model to the four-switch buck-boost, and from there to a design that works across the whole range.

This trail starts from what the buck trail has already built and covers only what changes in the boost and the buck-boost: the plant with the right-half-plane zero, the sample that reads the ESR, and a compensator that must work over a range of operating points. The general notes of the buck trail apply here unchanged and remain as detours: Sampling and the zero-order hold (ZOH), The bilinear (Tustin) transform and prewarping, ADC quantization, Q15 fixed point and The 2p2z in C, in the interrupt. The worked examples are the Synchronous boost 12 V → 48 V: worked example and the Four-switch buck-boost 8–16 V → 12 V: worked example.
