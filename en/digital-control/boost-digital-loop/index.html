From the boost to the digital loop

What changes in the digital loop when the plant has a right-half-plane zero: from the boost's averaged model to the four-switch buck-boost, and from there to a design that works across the whole range.

This trail starts from what the buck trail has already built and covers only what changes in the boost and the buck-boost: the plant with the right-half-plane zero, the sample that reads the ESR, and a compensator that must work over a range of operating points. The general notes of the buck trail apply here unchanged and remain as detours: Sampling and the zero-order hold (ZOH), The bilinear (Tustin) transform and prewarping, ADC quantization, Q15 fixed point and The 2p2z in C, in the interrupt. The worked examples are the Synchronous boost 12 V → 48 V: worked example and the Four-switch buck-boost 8–16 V → 12 V: worked example.

  3. The right-half-plane zero: the term the buck does not have creates a right-half-plane zero and limits the crossover.
  4. Loop gain, crossover frequency and margins: with the new plant, the loop gain and the margins, now at more than one operating point.
  5. Computation and PWM delay: the firmware's delay adds lost phase to the zero's.
  6. Sampling instant and the ESR in the boost: the sampling instant changes the plant the ADC sees and the effective delay.
  7. ADC trigger and sample instant: on a board, the ADC trigger moves away from the edge, and the boost needs care about which side of G1's edge it lands on.
  8. Measuring the loop gain by injecting into the duty: the loop gain measured in the switched circuit checks the sampled-data model.
  9. Four-switch buck-boost: the same loop in the four-switch buck-boost, whose plant changes with the input.
  10. Robust design across the operating range: one fixed compensator for the whole input and load range.
  11. The 2p2z compensator: the boost's 2p2z, with the zeros below the resonance and a low crossover.
  12. DPWM resolution and limit cycles: with a plant gain eight times larger, the DPWM step weighs more.
  13. Anti-windup and duty saturation: steps without a slew saturate the duty, and the firmware's form decides the outcome.
  14. Derivative kick and derivative on the measurement: what saturates the duty on those steps is the derivative kick, and differentiating the measurement removes it.
  15. Mini-project: the firmware of one converter: write and check the firmware of one of the two converters, with the note's checklist.
