The bilinear (Tustin) transform and prewarping

How to take a controller from s to z by the trapezoidal rule, why the frequencies warp, how to correct them at the crossover, and how Tustin compares with Euler and with matched poles and zeros.

First, see: Sampling and the zero-order hold (ZOH)

We design the compensator in sss, with the Bode plot of the loop gain, but the firmware can only compute with samples: sums of present and past values, that is, a function of zzz. The exact relation is z=esTsz = e^{sT_s}z=esTs​, but s=ln⁡(z)/Tss = \ln(z)/T_ss=ln(z)/Ts​ is not a ratio of polynomials and does not become a difference equation. We need an approximation.

Three approximations of sss

They all come from approximating an integral ∫x dt\int x\,dt∫xdt over one period:

  • Forward Euler, a rectangle on the old sample: s≈(z−1)/Tss \approx (z-1)/T_ss≈(z−1)/Ts​.
  • Backward Euler, a rectangle on the new sample: s≈(z−1)/(zTs)s \approx (z-1)/(zT_s)s≈(z−1)/(zTs​).
  • Tustin, a trapezoid between the two:
s=2Ts z−1z+1=2Ts 1−z−11+z−1(1)s = \frac{2}{T_s}\,\frac{z - 1}{z + 1} = \frac{2}{T_s}\,\frac{1 - z^{-1}}{1 + z^{-1}}\tag{1}s=Ts​2​z+1z−1​=Ts​2​1+z−11−z−1​(1)
≡

Tustin is the only one of the three that maps the whole left half of the s-plane inside the unit circle and the jωj\omegajω axis exactly onto the circle. Backward Euler also preserves stability, but maps the jωj\omegajω axis onto a smaller circle inside the unit one; forward Euler can push a stable pole out of the circle. With Tustin, a stable continuous controller stays stable, and the frequency response is the same, only with the frequency axis warped.

Frequency warping and prewarping

Since the whole jωj\omegajω axis, out to infinity, is wrapped onto the circle up to fs/2f_s/2fs​/2, the frequencies are compressed. The frequency ωd\omega_dωd​ of the discrete controller reproduces what the continuous prototype did at ωa\omega_aωa​:

Well below fs/2f_s/2fs​/2, tan⁡x≈x\tan x \approx xtanx≈x and the warping vanishes. Near fs/2f_s/2fs​/2, it is strong. When one frequency matters, such as the crossover, the constant 2/Ts2/T_s2/Ts​ is replaced with ω0/tan⁡(ω0Ts/2)\omega_0/\tan(\omega_0 T_s/2)ω0​/tan(ω0​Ts​/2), the second part of (2): the discrete response then agrees with the continuous one exactly at ω0\omega_0ω0​.

Prewarping

It is choosing the bilinear transform's constant so that a chosen frequency ω0\omega_0ω0​ is reproduced without error. The others remain warped, less so near ω0\omega_0ω0​.

Matched poles and zeros

Another way is not to approximate sss, but to map each pole and each zero by the exact relation: a root at s=−ωs = -\omegas=−ω goes to z=e−ωTsz = e^{-\omega T_s}z=e−ωTs​, and the integrator goes to z=1z = 1z=1. The gain is chosen afterwards, to cross where we want. This is the method of this site's 2p2z compensators (The 2p2z compensator): the Bode window computes KKK so that ∣TL∣=1|T_L| = 1∣TL​∣=1 at the requested frequency, with the discrete controller already in place. The table of discretizations puts the methods side by side.

Forward Euler with fast poles

Forward Euler maps s=−ωs = -\omegas=−ω to z=1−ωTsz = 1 - \omega T_sz=1−ωTs​. With ωTs>2\omega T_s > 2ωTs​>2, the pole leaves the unit circle: a perfectly stable continuous filter becomes an unstable controller. In the buck, a pole at 100 kHz would give z=1−2π×105×5×10−6=−2.14z = 1 - 2\pi \times 10^5 \times 5 \times 10^{-6} = -2.14z=1−2π×105×5×10−6=−2.14.

In the converters

The buck's 2p2z has double zeros at 2 kHz, a pole at 60 kHz and crosses at 8 kHz, with Ts=5T_s = 5Ts​=5 µs (200 kHz). Comparing the discretizations with the same integral gain as the continuous prototype, at 8 kHz:

Matching loses 2° against the continuous prototype; since the site's 41° margin is computed with the C(z)C(z)C(z) the firmware runs, that loss is already in it. The two Euler methods are off by 6° in opposite directions.
