Crossover and margins

  • Crossover frequency fcf_cfc​: where ∣T∣=1|T| = 1∣T∣=1 (0 dB). In order of magnitude, it is the closed loop's bandwidth; the higher it is, the faster the loop corrects.
  • Phase margin (MF): 180∘+∠T(j2πfc)180^\circ + \angle T(j2\pi f_c)180∘+∠T(j2πfc​). It is how much extra phase lag the loop still tolerates at fcf_cfc​ before it oscillates.
  • Gain margin (MG): −∣T∣dB-|T|_{\mathrm{dB}}−∣T∣dB​ at the frequency where the phase crosses −180∘-180^\circ−180∘. It is how much the gain can still rise.

In the time domain, with an MF of 60° there is no peak, the overshoot is small and the ringing dies out in a few cycles of fcf_cfc​; with 30°, the peak is close to 6 dB, and every step rings near fcf_cfc​. A rough rule for the damping of the pole pair near the crossover is ζ≈MF/100\zeta \approx \mathrm{MF}/100ζ≈MF/100 (From margin to damping: the ζ ≈ MF/100 rule and where it fails); with the 2p2z, it misses by up to 11 % in the boost (12 V, 1 and 2 A), up to 24 % in the buck-boost (12 V at 1 and 2 A, 9 V at 3 A) and 31 % in the buck; at other points of the buck-boost, by more: 27 % at 8 V and 3 A, 36.5 % at 9 V and 1 A. In all three converters, this pair rules the load step, but it is not dominant: there is also a slow real pole, near 470 Hz in the buck, 200 Hz in the boost and 330 Hz in the buck-boost, which makes the tail and sets the settling when the reference changes.

Why fcf_cfc​ cannot rise at will

Three things limit the crossover of a converter controlled by a microcontroller:

  1. Sampling. The firmware sees the output once per period (Sampling and the zero-order hold (ZOH)). Nothing above fs/2f_s/2fs​/2 can be controlled, and well before that the averaged model loses accuracy.
  2. Delay. The duty computed from a sample only moves the output (1+D) Ts(1+D)\,T_s(1+D)Ts​ later (Computation and PWM delay). This delay leaves ∣T∣|T|∣T∣ alone, but takes a phase that grows linearly with fff, and above the LC resonance the buck's phase is already near −180∘-180^\circ−180∘. At fs/10f_s/10fs​/10, the buck's delay costs (1+D)⋅36∘(1+D)\cdot 36^\circ(1+D)⋅36∘ (49.5°), more than the compensator gives back (The 2p2z compensator): in practice, fcf_cfc​ seldom goes above fs/10f_s/10fs​/10. The buck's 2p2z crosses at fs/25f_s/25fs​/25, where the delay costs 19.8°.
  3. The right-half-plane zero, in the boost and the buck-boost: it adds phase lag like a pole while it raises the gain, and fcf_cfc​ must stay well below it (The right-half-plane zero).
