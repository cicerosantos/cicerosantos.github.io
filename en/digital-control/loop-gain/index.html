Crossover and margins

  • Crossover frequency fcf_cfc​: where ∣TL∣=1|T_L| = 1∣TL​∣=1 (0 dB). In order of magnitude, it is the closed loop's bandwidth; the higher it is, the faster the loop corrects.

That is what the margin predicts in the time domain: with a PM of 60° there is no peak, the overshoot is small and a step settles in a few cycles of fcf_cfc​. With 30°, the peak is already close to 6 dB, and every step rings at a frequency near fcf_cfc​. A rough rule for the damping of the dominant pair is ζ≈PM/100\zeta \approx \mathrm{PM}/100ζ≈PM/100.

Why fcf_cfc​ cannot rise at will

Three things limit the crossover of a converter controlled by a microcontroller:

  1. Sampling. The firmware sees the output once per period (Sampling and the zero-order hold (ZOH)). Nothing above fs/2f_s/2fs​/2 can be controlled, and well before that the averaged model loses accuracy.
  2. Delay. The duty computed from a sample only moves the output (1+D) Ts(1+D)\,T_s(1+D)Ts​ later (Computation and PWM delay). This delay leaves ∣TL∣|T_L|∣TL​∣ alone, but takes a phase that grows linearly with fff. Above the LC resonance, the buck's phase is already near −180∘-180^\circ−180∘, and the compensator only gives back a limited lead (The 2p2z compensator). So, in practice, fcf_cfc​ seldom goes above fs/10f_s/10fs​/10; the buck's 2p2z crosses at fs/25f_s/25fs​/25.
  3. The right-half-plane zero, in the boost and the buck-boost: it adds phase lag like a pole while it raises the gain, and fcf_cfc​ must stay well below it (The right-half-plane zero).

The margin at one operating point only

GvdG_{vd}Gvd​ changes with the load, the input voltage and the duty. Check the margins in the worst case, not only at the nominal point; in the boost, the worst case is the maximum load with the minimum input.
