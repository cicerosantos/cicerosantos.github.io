From the analog buck to the digital loop

From the power stage to the designed and measured digital loop of a microcontroller-controlled buck.

Start the trail →

This trail walks through the design of a buck converter controlled by a microcontroller, from the duty cycle to the designed and measured digital loop. It needs the From Laplace to Bode and Closing and compensating the loop trails, at least unit 2. Follow the steps in order; the links in each note are detours. The worked example is the Synchronous buck 24 V → 9 V: worked example. What changes when the plant has a right-half-plane zero comes next, in the From the boost to the digital loop trail, and the code that runs the loop, in From compensator to firmware.

  1. Duty ratio and ripple of the buck in CCM: the starting point, the duty cycle and the ripple in steady state.
  2. Sizing L and C: from the ripple specifications, we choose L and C.
  3. The averaged switch model: to control the converter, we replace the switch by its average.
  4. From circuit to factored form (reinforcement): optional where the plant's formulas come from, through the circuit; it needs RC and RL circuits: from the circuit to the pole-zero form, from the From circuit to transfer function trail.
  5. The buck's transfer function Gvd(s)G_{vd}(s)Gvd​(s): linearizing the averaged model gives the plant Gvd(s)G_{vd}(s)Gvd​(s).
  6. The ESR zero: the capacitor's series resistance adds a zero to the plant.
  7. Loop gain, crossover frequency and margins: plant and controller form the loop gain, and the margins tell whether the loop is stable.
  8. Sampling and the zero-order hold (ZOH): in the microcontroller, the output is sampled and the duty cycle is held for a period.
  9. Computation and PWM delay: computing and the PWM delay the response, and the delay costs phase margin.
  10. PWM update: trailing edge vs symmetric: the delay depends on when the update event copies the new duty cycle, with a trailing edge or a symmetric PWM, and the two ways of counting the delay do not mix.
  11. Sensing and anti-aliasing filter: the filter in front of the ADC adds a little more delay, and we choose its pole, measuring the margin with the 2p2z of Loop gain, crossover frequency and margins (detailed in step 13).
  12. From the s-plane to the z-plane: before discretizing, we see where sampling takes each pole: z=esTsz = e^{sT_s}z=esTs​, the unit circle and the buck's poles near z=1z = 1z=1.
  13. The bilinear (Tustin) transform and prewarping: we take the controller from s to z.
  14. The 2p2z compensator: the two-pole, two-zero compensator, designed from its poles and zeros.
  15. ADC quantization: the ADC sees only steps of voltage.
  16. DPWM resolution and limit cycles: the duty cycle moves in steps too, which can cause limit cycles.
  17. Measuring the loop gain by injecting into the duty: we measure the loop gain in the switched circuit, with a sine large enough to beat those steps.

End of the trail

The loop is designed and measured. Next, the From the boost to the digital loop trail shows what changes with the right-half-plane zero; then From compensator to firmware takes the loop to the board's code.
