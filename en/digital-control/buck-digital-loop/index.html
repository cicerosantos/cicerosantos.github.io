From the analog buck to the digital loop

From the power stage to the firmware: the design path of a microcontroller-controlled buck, note by note.

This trail walks through the design of a buck converter controlled by a microcontroller, from the duty cycle to the interrupt's code. Follow the steps in order; the links inside each note are optional detours. The worked example is the Synchronous buck 24 V → 9 V: worked example.

  1. Duty ratio and ripple of the buck in CCM: the starting point, the duty cycle and the ripple in steady state.
  2. Sizing L and C: from the ripple specifications, we choose L and C.
  3. The averaged switch model: to control the converter, we replace the switch by its average.
  4. The buck's transfer function Gvd(s)G_{vd}(s)Gvd​(s): linearizing the averaged model gives the plant Gvd(s)G_{vd}(s)Gvd​(s).
  5. The ESR zero: the capacitor's series resistance adds a zero to the plant.
  6. Loop gain, crossover frequency and margins: plant and controller form the loop gain, and the margins tell whether the loop is stable.
  7. Sampling and the zero-order hold (ZOH): in the microcontroller, the output is sampled and the duty cycle is held for a period.
  8. Computation and PWM delay: computing and the PWM delay the response, and the delay costs phase margin.
  9. The bilinear (Tustin) transform and prewarping: we take the controller from s to z.
  10. The 2p2z compensator: the two-pole, two-zero compensator, designed from its poles and zeros.
  11. ADC quantization: the ADC sees only steps of voltage.
  12. Measuring the loop gain by injecting into the duty: we measure the loop gain in the switched circuit, with a sine large enough to beat those steps.
  13. DPWM resolution and limit cycles: the duty cycle moves in steps too, which can cause limit cycles.
  14. Q15 fixed point: without an FPU, numbers become scaled integers.
  15. Anti-windup and duty saturation: when the duty cycle saturates, the controller must stop accumulating error.
  16. The 2p2z in C, in the interrupt: everything together, in the interrupt's C code.
