Centered PWM on the H7: case B

Centered pulse from two comparators of the up-counter, sample at the reset and load at the next reset: the delay to the pulse center is 1.5 Ts1.5\,T_s1.5Ts​, whatever DDD.

First, see: PWM update: trailing edge vs symmetric, Computation and PWM delay

The STM32H7 HRTIM has no up-down counting mode, the one that centers the pulse in symmetric PWM. The reference manual (RM0433) gives only the up-counter, so the centered pulse comes from two of its comparators: CMP1 sets the output and CMP2 resets it. Each period, the CPU recalculates both from DDD:

c1=(1−D) Ts2,c2=(1+D) Ts2,c1+c2=Ts.c_1 = \frac{(1-D)\,T_s}{2}, \qquad c_2 = \frac{(1+D)\,T_s}{2}, \qquad c_1 + c_2 = T_s.c1​=2(1−D)Ts​​,c2​=2(1+D)Ts​​,c1​+c2​=Ts​.

For D=0.375D = 0.375D=0.375 and Ts=5 µsT_s = 5\ \text{µs}Ts​=5 µs, c1=1.5625 µsc_1 = 1.5625\ \text{µs}c1​=1.5625 µs and c2=3.4375 µsc_2 = 3.4375\ \text{µs}c2​=3.4375 µs. The center of the pulse stays at Ts/2T_s/2Ts​/2 for any DDD, because the two edges move in opposite directions.

Case B: the sequence

The figure shows two periods, kkk and k+1k+1k+1, with time counted from the sample (the reset of period kkk). The other timings are in PWM update: trailing edge vs symmetric.

  1. The counter climbs from 0 to PER and restarts at every period.
  2. The ADC is triggered by the period event: the sample falls on the reset of period kkk. CMP2 does not trigger the sample.
  3. The CPU converts, computes DDD and writes CMP1 and CMP2 into the preload.
  4. The update event at the next reset, that of k+1k+1k+1, copies both values into the active registers.
  5. In period k+1k+1k+1, CMP1 sets the output at 1.5625 µs1.5625\ \text{µs}1.5625 µs and CMP2 resets it at 3.4375 µs3.4375\ \text{µs}3.4375 µs: the pulse is centered at 2.5 µs2.5\ \text{µs}2.5 µs.

The delay is 1.5·Tₛ for any D

From the sample to the update event there is one period, TsT_sTs​; from there to the pulse center, half a period, Ts/2T_s/2Ts​/2. The total, 1.5 Ts1.5\,T_s1.5Ts​, does not depend on DDD, because the center does not move when DDD changes. It is the "ZOH plus z−1z^{-1}z−1" count of PWM update: trailing edge vs symmetric, with 1.5 Ts1.5\,T_s1.5Ts​ as the nominal value. The effective sample falls at the end of the sampling window, tLATR+tSt_{LATR} + t_StLATR​+tS​ after the reset. With the ADC at 50 MHz, tLATR is 1.5 to 2.5 cycles (30 to 50 ns), and the window is 8.5 cycles (170 ns, see below): the sample falls 200200200 to 220 ns220\ \text{ns}220 ns after the reset, and the delay to the pulse center is between 7.287.287.28 and 7.30 µs7.30\ \text{µs}7.30 µs.

The window is not the register minimum, 1.5 cycles (30 ns; DS12110, table 174, silicon revision V). At 12 bits, table 175 (p. 276) asks for at least 53 ns on a direct channel, 80 ns on a fast channel and 129 ns on a slow channel, even with RAINR_{AIN}RAIN​ = 47 Ω. They hold up to 125 °C, with 47 pF on the board and VDDAV_{DDA}VDDA​ = 1.6 V. The H7's SMP field only takes 1.5, 2.5, 8.5, 16.5, 32.5, 64.5, 387.5 and 810.5 cycles (RM0433, section 25.4.13). With a direct channel and a source of RAIN≤47R_{AIN} \le 47RAIN​≤47 Ω (the divider with a buffer), as this note assumes, 2.5 cycles (50 ns) are not enough, and the smallest value that works is 8.5 cycles.

Computation budget

The write to the preload has to arrive before the update event, that is, within TsT_sTs​ after the sample. In that interval fit the latency of the ADC trigger from the HRTIM, the conversion and the computation: the budget is TsT_sTs​ minus those three. None of them is measured yet. The latency and the conversion come from the ADC datasheet (DS12110, table 174, silicon revision V), since RM0433 does not give the latency; the computation has to be measured in the H7 firmware. That is why the figure has no numbers on those bars.

The current and the sample

The inductor current equals its average at two instants of each period: at the reset, which falls at the midpoint of the fall, and at the pulse center, which falls at the midpoint of the rise. With the trigger latency and the 8.5-cycle window, the sample falls 200 to 220 ns after the reset, still on the fall, whose slope is 9 V/22 µH=0.409 A/µs9\ \text{V}/22\ \text{µH} = 0.409\ \text{A/µs}9 V/22 µH=0.409 A/µs: the sample sits 82 to 90 mA below the average (model result). The dead time of 50 ns in the model shifts the pulse center by about +25 ns and the current at the reset by about +10 mA, measured in QSPICE (the model gives +9 mA), and the current at T/2T/2T/2 falls by about 17 mA; the figure does not show this effect. The output voltage does not fare as well: in the buck example with D=0.375D = 0.375D=0.375, the reset falls at the peak of the vCv_CvC​ ripple, and the sampled voltage deviates from the average by +7.84 mV at the reset and by −9.21 mV at the pulse center, measured in QSPICE (the ideal model gives +7.79 mV and −9.21 mV), where the steady-state average sits 8.7 mV below the reference; the ideal model does not predict this offset, which comes from the losses and the dead time, and it does not change the offsets relative to the average; the figure does not draw it. The trigger latency still needs a measurement on the board.

Open items

These figures need validation before they count as fact: the ADC trigger latency (tLATR of 1.5 to 2.5 cycles with CKMODE = 00) and the conversion time (DS12110, table 174), the sampling window (table 175, which depends on the channel and on the source impedance), the computation time measured on the H7, the comparison and preload rules, which are inferred from RM0433, and the voltage offset and the margin with case B, measured in QSPICE. Under the H7 migration rule, an independent reviewer checks the numbers before publication.
