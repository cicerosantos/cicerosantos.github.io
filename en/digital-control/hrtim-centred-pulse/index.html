Centered PWM on the H7: case B

Centered pulse from two comparators of the up-counter, sample at the reset and load at the next reset: the delay to the pulse center is 1.5 Ts1.5\,T_s1.5Ts​, whatever DDD.

First, see: PWM update: trailing edge vs symmetric, Computation and PWM delay

The STM32H7 HRTIM has no counting mode that goes up and down, the one that centers the pulse in symmetric PWM. The reference manual (RM0433) gives only the up-counter, so the centered pulse comes from two comparators of that same counter: CMP1 sets the output and CMP2 resets it. Each period, the CPU recalculates both from DDD:

c1=(1−D) Ts2,c2=(1+D) Ts2,c1+c2=Ts.c_1 = \frac{(1-D)\,T_s}{2}, \qquad c_2 = \frac{(1+D)\,T_s}{2}, \qquad c_1 + c_2 = T_s.c1​=2(1−D)Ts​​,c2​=2(1+D)Ts​​,c1​+c2​=Ts​.

Case B: the sequence

The figure shows two periods, kkk and k+1k+1k+1, with time counted from the sample (the reset of period kkk). It is illustrative and shows this case only; PWM update: trailing edge vs symmetric covers the other timings.

  1. The counter climbs from 0 to PER and restarts at every period.
  2. The ADC is triggered by the period event: the sample falls on the reset of period kkk. The trigger is not a comparator, and CMP2 does not trigger the sample.
  3. The CPU converts, computes DDD and writes CMP1 and CMP2 into the preload.
  4. The update event at the next reset, that of k+1k+1k+1, copies both values into the active registers.
  5. In period k+1k+1k+1, CMP1 sets the output at 1.5625 µs1.5625\ \text{µs}1.5625 µs and CMP2 resets it at 3.4375 µs3.4375\ \text{µs}3.4375 µs: the pulse is centered at 2.5 µs2.5\ \text{µs}2.5 µs.

Computation budget

The write to the preload has to arrive before the update event, that is, within TsT_sTs​ after the sample. In that interval fit the latency of the ADC trigger from the HRTIM, the conversion and the computation: the budget is TsT_sTs​ minus those three. None of them is measured yet. The latency and the conversion come from the ADC datasheet (DS12110, table 77), since RM0433 does not give that latency, and the computation has to be measured in the H7 firmware. That is why the figure has no numbers on those bars.

The current and the sample

The inductor current equals its average at two instants of each period: at the reset, which falls at the midpoint of the fall, and at the pulse center, which falls at the midpoint of the rise. At the ideal reset, the current is the average. With the trigger latency and the minimum sampling time of the ADC, the sample falls 35 to 46 mA below the average (model result). The dead time of 50 ns in the model shifts the pulse center by about +25 ns and the current at the reset by about +10 mA, measured in QSPICE (the model gives +9 mA), and the current at T/2T/2T/2 falls by about 17 mA; the figure does not show this effect. The output voltage does not fare as well: in the buck example with D=0.375D = 0.375D=0.375, the reset falls at the peak of the vCv_CvC​ ripple, and the sampled voltage deviates from the average by +7.84 mV at the reset and by −9.21 mV at the pulse center, measured in QSPICE (the ideal model gives +7.79 mV and −9.21 mV). In QSPICE, the steady-state average sits 8.7 mV below the reference; the ideal model does not predict this offset, which comes from the losses and the dead time, and it does not change the offsets relative to the average. The figure does not draw this offset; the offsets relative to the average were measured in QSPICE; the trigger latency still needs a measurement on the board.

Open items

These figures need validation before they count as fact: the trigger latency to the ADC (DS12110, table 77: tLATR of 1.5 to 2.5 cycles; RM0433 does not give this value) and the conversion time (same table), the computation time measured on the H7, the comparison and preload rules, which are inferred from RM0433, and the voltage offset and the margin with case B, measured in QSPICE. Under the H7 migration rule, an independent reviewer checks the numbers before publication.
