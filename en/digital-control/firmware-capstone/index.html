Mini-project: the firmware of one converter

Write and check in C the digital loop of the boost or the buck-boost, with a checklist, two calculations with answers, and a delivery sheet with the expected values.

First, see: The 2p2z in C, in the interrupt, Anti-windup and duty saturation, Robust design across the operating range

This is the final step of the digital control trail, a mini-project. You choose one of the two converters with a right-half-plane zero, the boost or the buck-boost, write its digital loop in C, and check the code against the numbers this site already records.

Checklist

Check each item in your code before running any simulation.

  • Coefficients. b0b_0b0​, b1b_1b1​, b2b_2b2​ and ppp are those of the table in the last section, copied from scenarios.json with every digit. In the code, a1=−(1+p)a_1 = -(1+p)a1​=−(1+p) and a2=pa_2 = pa2​=p (The 2p2z compensator).
  • State. The firmware stores the duty actually applied, after the limit, not the computed one. That prevents accumulation beyond the limit, but not the return of the kick: in the velocity form, the term b1 e[k−1]b_1\,e[k-1]b1​e[k−1] of the next period removes the jump that was never applied, and on the boost the duty drops to 0.09 and sits at zero for two periods (Anti-windup and duty saturation).
  • Reference step. Change the reference along a ramp (Soft-start and pre-biasing) or move the derivative to the measurement (Derivative kick and derivative on the measurement), so the error does not jump.
  • Limits and reference. The duty stays between 0 and DmaxD_{max}Dmax​, and REF_CODE is a rounded integer, not a truncated one (ADC quantization).
  • Sample. The ADC triggers at the period start, just before G1 turns on, as in the scenarios; the coefficients hold for that instant. Sampling within G1 calls for redoing the plant (ADC trigger and sample instant).
  • Start-up. The recorded scenarios begin in steady state; test start-up and protections separately.
