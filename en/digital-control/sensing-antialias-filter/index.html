Sensing and anti-aliasing filter

The RC between the divider and the ADC: the pole fpf_pfp​, the phase it takes at the crossover, the time constant the sample sees, and how to choose fpf_pfp​ between fcf_cfc​ and fs/2f_s/2fs​/2 when sampling is already synchronous.

First, see: Sampling and the zero-order hold (ZOH), Computation and PWM delay, First-order pole and zero

In the site's scenarios, the ADC reads the output with no filter. On a board, there is almost always a capacitor CfC_fCf​ between the divider and the ADC pin. With the divider's equivalent resistance, RRR, it forms a first-order low-pass:

Two different RCs

There are two RCs in the measurement path, with opposite rules.

  1. The acquisition RC. During the sampling window, the ADC's internal capacitor, CshC_{sh}Csh​, 4 pF on the STM32H7 considered in ADC trigger and sample instant, charges through the source impedance. That time constant must fit about 9 times into the 170 ns window, for half an LSB at 12 bits.
  2. The sensing RC, R CfR\,C_fRCf​, with CfC_fCf​ in nanofarads and τ\tauτ in microseconds. It does not settle within the window, and it need not: during the window, CfC_fCf​ is the low-impedance source that charges the internal capacitor. The error becomes charge sharing, Csh/(Cf+Csh)C_{sh}/(C_f + C_{sh})Csh​/(Cf​+Csh​) times the difference between the voltage the internal capacitor held and the pin's; in the worst case, a full-scale jump. This calls for a low-impedance divider: with 50 kΩ, the same pole at 80 kHz leaves CfC_fCf​ at 40 pF, and charge sharing exceeds 9 %; then the remedy is a buffer.

What it has to reject

Synchronous sampling already takes care of the ripple: it sits at fsf_sfs​ and its harmonics, which fold to zero frequency and become the sampling offset (Sampling and the zero-order hold (ZOH)). The filter only changes that offset a little. Two things remain:

  • Switching spikes. Right after each edge, the measurement rings at tens of MHz. A sample near the edge, with a few nanoseconds of jitter, catches a different point of the ringing every period, and the error becomes noise.
  • Broadband noise, coming from the driver and coupled through the layout. Whatever sits near kfs±fk f_s \pm fkfs​±f folds to fff, inside the loop's band.

With fc=fs/25f_c = f_s/25fc​=fs​/25, a first-order low-pass is not a real anti-aliasing filter at fs/2f_s/2fs​/2 without eating into the margin: in the buck, 192 kHz folds to 8 kHz, and the pole at 80 kHz attenuates it only 8.3 dB. Those components are avoided at the source, with the layout and the sampling instant. CfC_fCf​ sits right at the pin, with its return to the microcontroller's analog ground; a spike that comes in through the ground is not filtered by the RC, and the remedy is the layout, or a differential measurement with a Kelvin connection at the load point.

How to choose fpf_pfp​

The floor is the phase at fcf_cfc​; the ceiling, the attenuation the spikes need, in MHz, not at fs/2f_s/2fs​/2. For the buck (200 kHz, fcf_cfc​ = 8 kHz), with the pole included in the site's model:

The active version: Sallen-Key

A Butterworth Sallen-Key low-pass with f0f_0f0​ = 80 kHz falls 40 dB per decade, but takes 8.1° at 8 kHz, against 5.7° for the RC at the same f0f_0f0​, and leaves the 2p2z with 33.2°. Above the amplifier's bandwidth, the attenuation stops growing, far from the −96 dB the ideal model gives at 20 MHz; on top of that come the amplifier's offset and noise. A small RC between the amplifier and the pin absorbs the kick of the internal capacitor's charge and cuts what leaks through.

The scenarios' loops keep the RC: the ripple already disappears through synchronous sampling, the spikes are more than two decades above fpf_pfp​, where 48 dB is enough, and the active pair would only win in theory.

In the converters

Buck. With RRR = 1 kΩ, CfC_fCf​ ≈ 2 nF puts fpf_pfp​ at 80 kHz, and the 2p2z goes from 41.3° to 35.7°. In the aggressive design, which crosses at 18 kHz, the same pole takes 12.7°: from 15.5° to 4.0°.

Boost. With the crossover at 1.5 kHz, a pole at 15 kHz costs 5.7°, moves the 2p2z, at 1 A, from 46.2° to 40.4° and attenuates 10.8 dB at fs/2f_s/2fs​/2 = 50 kHz.

Buck-boost. With the crossover at 3 kHz, the pole at 30 kHz takes the 2p2z from 42.6° to 37.0°.

Exercise

The buck's ADC measures only the output, with CfC_fCf​ = 2 nF and a 4 pF internal capacitor. What fraction of the difference between consecutive samples becomes error through charge sharing? And if the same ADC alternated with a channel sitting at the other end of the scale?

Answer

4/(2000+4)=0.24/(2000 + 4) = 0.24/(2000+4)=0.2 %. Between consecutive samples of the output, the difference is millivolts: negligible error. On a full-scale jump, 0.2 % of 4096 steps is 8.2 steps: the error shows. To stay below half a step at 12 bits, CfC_fCf​ would have to exceed (213−1)×4(2^{13} - 1) \times 4(213−1)×4 pF ≈ 33 nF; with RRR = 1 kΩ, that brings fpf_pfp​ down to 4.8 kHz, below fcf_cfc​. What is left is the buffer, or converting the output channel always right after a channel at a nearby voltage.
