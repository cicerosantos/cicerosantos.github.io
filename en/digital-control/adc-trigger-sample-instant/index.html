ADC trigger and sample instant

From the timer's trigger to the instant the ADC actually samples, and where to put that instant in the period: the offset from the average, the ESR term in the plant and the (1+D−φ) Ts(1 + D - \varphi)\,T_s(1+D−φ)Ts​ delay.

First, see: Computation and PWM delay, ADC quantization, Sampling instant and the ESR in the boost

The site's scenarios sample the output at the start of the period, just before G1 turns on. On a board, the sample instant is a choice: the timer triggers the ADC, the ADC takes a while to sample, and the point chosen changes the value read, the plant and the delay.

The trigger

The PWM timer triggers the ADC in hardware. Between the trigger and the result there are three times, on the H7:

  1. Trigger latency, tLATRt_{LATR}tLATR​. The trigger reaches the ADC 1.5 to 2.5 ADC cycles later, in asynchronous mode (CKMODE = 00; in the synchronous modes, up to 2.5, 2.5 and 2.25 cycles).
  2. Sampling window, tSt_StS​. It comes from table 175 of DS12110 (silicon revision V), not from the register minimum: 8.5 cycles, 170 ns at 50 MHz, on a direct channel with a low-impedance source (Centered PWM on the H7: case B).
  3. Conversion. At 12 bits, 8.5+0.5+12/2=158.5 + 0.5 + 12/2 = 158.5+0.5+12/2=15 cycles: 300 ns with the ADC at 50 MHz, the maximum with BOOST = 11 (table 174).

The effective sample instant is the end of the window, tLATR+tSt_{LATR} + t_StLATR​+tS​: 200 to 220 ns after the trigger, at 50 MHz. This delay does not enter (1+D) Ts(1+D)\,T_s(1+D)Ts​ (Computation and PWM delay): in the worst case, it adds 0.63° at 8 kHz. The ADC clock is a design variable, up to 50 MHz.

High-impedance divider

The internal sampling capacitor, 4 pF (DS12110, table 174), charges through the divider during the window. Table 175 gives the minimum window for each external impedance and, at 12 bits, only goes up to 680 Ω (301 ns on a direct channel). With a 50 kΩ divider, the RC constant is 200 ns, more than the whole 170 ns window: the capacitor doesn't charge fully, and the code comes out low. To settle within half an LSB at 12 bits, the window needs ln⁡213≈9.0\ln 2^{13} \approx 9.0ln213≈9.0 RC constants: about 1.8 µs. At 50 MHz, the first SMP that covers it is 387.5 cycles (RM0433): 7.75 µs, more than the buck's 5 µs period. A longer window may not fit: the fix is a buffer. A capacitor at the pin only works with a low-impedance divider; with 50 kΩ, it cannot be large enough without putting the pole in the loop's band (Sensing and anti-aliasing filter).

Where to sample

With synchronous sampling, the ripple becomes a constant offset between the sample and the average, at any instant (Sampling and the zero-order hold (ZOH)). The loop regulates the sample, and the average is shifted. The offset depends on the instant, in the switched circuit's steady state:

Sample offset (mV) Edge Middle of G1 Middle of G2
Buck, 9 V and 1 A −6.0 −9.2 +7.8
Boost, 1 A +52.9 −11.4 +37.3
Boost, 2 A +121.7 −21.4 +68.2
Buck-boost, 12 V and 2 A +51.6 −25.6 +31.8

"Peak and valley give the average" holds for iLi_LiL​, not for vov_ovo​: no instant cancels the voltage offset.

In the simulation, the edge is clean. On the board, right after the edge the measured voltage rings for tens to hundreds of nanoseconds, from the layout's parasitic inductances and the MOSFETs' capacitances: it is the worst instant. Trigger the ADC from a compare channel of the timer (PWM timer and shadow registers), away from the edges, and check it on the scope.

In the boost and the buck-boost, "after the edge" is already the G1 interval. The sample loses the ESR term and jumps by RESRR_{ESR}RESR​ times the valley of iLi_LiL​: 27 mV at 1 A and 68 mV at 2 A, in the boost. In scenario.cpp, G2 turns off at t=0t = 0t=0, and G2's body diode takes the current: a soft edge. G1 turns on 100 ns later against that diode, with reverse recovery: a hard edge. A trigger at the period start samples 200 to 220 ns later, already inside G1. Advance the trigger so the sample falls before G1 turns on, next to G2's turn-off, or sample inside G1 after the ringing and redo the plant.

What changes in the plant

At the edge, the sample reads the ESR term of Sampling instant and the ESR in the boost, and the right-half-plane zero seen by the ADC moves up: from 14.1 to 22.1 kHz at 1 A and from 6.9 to 8.4 kHz at 2 A, in the boost. In the middle of G1, the ESR carries the constant current −io-i_o−io​: the coefficient of i^L\hat i_Li^L​ changes sign, from +RESR D+R_{ESR}\,D+RESR​D to −RESR D′-R_{ESR}\,D'−RESR​D′, that of d^\hat dd^ does not change, and the zero goes back to the average's. Compared with the sample at the edge, the 2p2z's margin loses about 2° to this term, and the shorter delay gives back a part, which the notes do not compute. In exchange, the ADC trigger has to follow the duty, with a second compare channel of the timer, and less time is left for the computation. Designing with the average's zero stays on the conservative side.

Delay with φ\varphiφ

With preload kept, moving the sample shortens the delay. In scenario.cpp, which simulates the boost and the buck-boost, SAMPLE_PHASE = φ\varphiφ is the fraction of the period from the start to the sample: 0, the default and the value of every published scenario, is the edge; 0.5 is the middle of the period. The delay to the falling edge becomes

td=(1+D−φ) Ts,t_d = (1 + D - \varphi)\,T_s,td​=(1+D−φ)Ts​,

provided the computation ends before the update event. The models behind the Bode plots hold only with φ=0\varphi = 0φ=0; another phase requires redoing the plant and recording the scenarios again.

Updating the CCR in the same period, without preload, would also cut the delay, but the write can arrive after the counter has passed the new value, and on the HRTIM the switch stays on until the next period (The 2p2z in C, in the interrupt).
