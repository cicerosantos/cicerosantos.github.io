Measuring the loop gain by injecting into the duty

A sine added to the duty in the firmware gives the loop gain as −C/U-C/U−C/U; the amplitude has to grow with frequency so the ADC sample moves several codes, and a second measurement at half the amplitude shows whether the point is linear.

First, see: Loop gain, crossover frequency and margins, ADC quantization

The site's models were checked against the switched circuit in QSPICE, with the scenarios' firmware. This note shows how: where the sine goes in, how the loop gain comes out, and the two precautions that separate a reliable measurement from one that only measures the ADC's rounding.

The injection

Inside the interrupt, the firmware adds a small sine xxx to the duty ccc the controller computed, and the PWM uses u=c+xu = c + xu=c+x. The sine goes around the loop: it moves the output, the ADC sample, the error and, one period later, ccc itself. With T=β Gc Gm GvdT = \beta\,G_c\,G_m\,G_{vd}T=βGc​Gm​Gvd​ and the error defined as reference minus sample, the part of ccc at the sine's frequency is c^=−T u^\hat c = -T\,\hat uc^=−Tu^, and

T=−CU,T = -\frac{C}{U},T=−UC​,

with CCC and UUU the Fourier components of ccc and uuu at the injected frequency. Since both signals are read at the same point in the firmware, the computation and PWM delays enter the measurement with no extra care. The same computation with the sample VVV in place of CCC gives the plant as the ADC sees it, V/UV/UV/U, without the compensator.

In the lab, tools/qspice_loop_check.py records ccc, uuu and the sample every period, waits 30 ms for the LC resonance, which rings for tens of milliseconds in open loop, and computes CCC and UUU over 40 whole cycles of the sine, with a Hann window against the leakage of that ringing. On a board, the procedure is the same: the firmware injects and records ccc and uuu, and the computation is done offline.

In the published scenarios, the injection never happens. TEST_MODE, a netlist parameter of the block (default 0), must be 1 for the injection to act; tools/qspice_loop_check.py sets it, and the published scenarios leave it at 0.

The amplitude grows with frequency

The sine has to be small so as not to leave the linear regime, but the ADC sample only moves in steps of q=VFS/(2N−1)q = V_{FS}/(2^N - 1)q=VFS​/(2N−1) (ADC quantization). Above the resonance, the plant falls, and a fixed amplitude that is enough at low frequency ends up moving the sample by less than one code: what gets measured then is the rounding, not the plant.

The way out is to choose, at each frequency, the amplitude that, according to the model, moves the sample by about 10 codes:

a(f)=min⁡ ⁣(0.06, max⁡ ⁣(0.004, 10 q∣P(f)∣)),a(f) = \min\!\left(0.06,\ \max\!\left(0.004,\ \frac{10\,q}{|P(f)|}\right)\right),a(f)=min(0.06, max(0.004, ∣P(f)∣10q​)),

with PPP the plant from the duty to the sample, in volts per unit of duty. The floor, 0.004, keeps the sine small near the resonance, where a large one would reverse the inductor current at light load; the ceiling is 0.06, or less when the applied duty, which swings by a/∣1+T∣a/|1+T|a/∣1+T∣, would come close to its limits: it may use at most 80 % of its distance to DminD_{min}Dmin​ or DmaxD_{max}Dmax​. In the boost at 10 V input, from 5.5 to 7 kHz, 0.06 would take the duty to 0.87, above the 0.85 limit.

The linearity check

In a linear system, the gain does not depend on the amplitude. So each frequency is measured again with half the sine: if TTT changes by more than 0.5 dB or 3°, the point is not linear and must not be used to check the model. The check catches the inductor current nearly reversing, the duty touching its limits and the sample moving only a few codes.

In the converters

Boost (qqq = 14.65 mV). At 1 A, the plant is 9.8 dB at 5.5 kHz and 5.7 dB at 7 kHz, about 3.1 and 1.9 V per unit of duty. With the old fixed sine of 0.004, the sample moved 0.84 and 0.52 code, and at half amplitude the response vanished: the check flagged −68 and −59 dB. With the growing amplitude, 0.047 at 5.5 kHz and 0.06 at 7 kHz (8 codes), the points pass the check, and the model stays within 0.2 dB and 1.4° of QSPICE from 300 Hz to 7 kHz, at 12 V and 1, 2 and 4 A.

Buck-boost (qqq = 4.884 mV). At 12 V and 1 A, the 0.004 sine moved the sample 2.4 codes at 7 kHz, and half the amplitude changed the gain by 0.9 dB. With 0.016, the change drops to 0.01 dB. At 12 V and 1 A, 12 V and 2 A, and 9 V and 3 A, the sampled-data model of Sampling instant and the ESR in the boost stays within 0.2 dB and 0.7° of QSPICE from 300 Hz to 7 kHz. The averaged model with the fitted delay (1+0.6 D) Ts(1 + 0.6\,D)\,T_s(1+0.6D)Ts​, which the site used before, stayed within 0.45 dB and 1.9°.

Buck (qqq = 4.884 mV). From 500 Hz to 15 kHz, the model with the (1+D) Ts(1 + D)\,T_s(1+D)Ts​ delay stays within 0.3 dB and 0.7° of QSPICE. At 30 kHz, the amplitude that would give 10 codes is above the ceiling; with 0.06, the magnitude matches the model (0.07 dB), but half the amplitude still changes the phase by 4.6°, and the point still fails. The cause is not the ADC: with 0.06, the inductor current swings by 0.41 A, more than the ripple's valley, 0.36 A, and briefly changes sign; the dead time then adds to the duty. With half the sine, the current does not reverse, and the measurement is within 0.3 dB and 0.7° of the model. No amplitude gives both enough codes and a current that does not reverse, so the point stays out of the validation. With the fixed sine, the magnitude there was 1.4 dB off.

Exercise 2

In the buck, the plant is −3.46 dB at 30 kHz. What amplitude would the rule ask for, which one does it use, and how many codes does the sample move with the full sine and with half of it?

Answer

∣P∣=10−3.46/20=0.671|P| = 10^{-3.46/20} = 0.671∣P∣=10−3.46/20=0.671, and the rule would ask for 10×0.004884/0.671=0.07310 \times 0.004884/0.671 = 0.07310×0.004884/0.671=0.073; the ceiling limits it to 0.06.
